Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to complete the Vocabulary Exercise 1: Environment activity from
EBA, in which they match the given environment-related words with the corresponding images
that represent these words. The teacher uses one of the web tools for matching activities to design
activities titled Environment and Sort the Waste. The teacher also creates a digital story titled Small
Steps, Big Changes utilising one of the animation creation tools (Annex-1). The teacher designs
another activity named Green Steps Game using one of the gamification web tools.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher implements the Environment matching activity as a warm-up. The students are asked to
drag and drop each keyword to its correct definition. The activity includes environmental words such
as energy, rubbish, pollution, and recycle.
While Activity
Following the matching activity, the teacher continues with the Sort the Waste activity. In this task, the
students are given words such as newspaper, jar, bottle, apple, etc., and are required to place them
into the correct recycling bin. This activity reinforces vocabulary related to waste materials, promotes
environmental responsibility, and allows the students to apply their knowledge in an interactive way.
After this activity, the teacher introduces the animation Small Steps, Big Changes (Annex-1). The
students listen to the animation, and the teacher asks them higher-order thinking questions to
encourage critical reflection, reasoning, and deeper understanding of the topic. To promote peer
interaction and collaboration, this stage can be conducted as pair or group work; after the teacher’s
initial questions, the students may continue by asking and answering questions with each other. For
example, the teacher may ask: ‘Why do you think recycling is important for our future?’ or ‘What can
people do if they forget to separate their waste?’
Post-Activity
The teacher implements the gamified exercise Green Steps Game as a wrap‑up activity, where the
students listen to sentences about eco-friendly actions and tap pairs of tiles to find the matching
ones. This enjoyable and dynamic game not only raises environmental awareness but also supports
listening comprehension and helps the students reinforce key vocabulary in an engaging way.

Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an activity titled Guess Game using one of the gamification web tools. The teacher
also creates an animation titled Save Our Planet using one of the animation creation tools (Annex-2).
Finally, the teacher uses one of the web tools for matching activities to prepare a matching activity
named Green Solutions.
2. Ders ( Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher implements the Guess Game to engage the students and activate their prior knowledge.
In this activity, the students examine a visual (for example, a picture of a forest) and try to guess the
word. They first say individual letters aloud and then attempt to predict and pronounce the complete
word correctly. This activity helps the students recall vocabulary and prepares them for the main part
of the lesson, as a warm-up.
While Activity
Following the warm-up activity, the teacher introduces the animation Save Our Planet (Annex-2). The
students watch and listen to the story, which includes key expressions such as: “We must recycle,” “We
have to save energy,” and “We shouldn’t waste water.” The teacher pauses at key moments during the
animation to ask short questions and actively involve students in the process. To make the activity
more engaging, the teacher can add a simple note-taking task during the animation. The students
can write short notes about what we should and shouldn’t do to protect the environment. They can use
their notes to write a few simple sentences before the question–answer activity. After watching, the
teacher asks higher-order thinking questions to encourage critical reflection, reasoning, and deeper
understanding of the topic, such as: “What can we do to save our planet?”, “Which eco-friendly habit
do you like most?”, “How can you help the Earth at home or at school?”, etc. Through these questions,
the students are encouraged to share their own ideas, reflect on daily habits, and use target language
structures such as: “We must” and “We have to” while expressing their thoughts about saving the
planet.
Post-Activity
The teacher moves on to the Green Solutions activity and asks the students to drag and drop each
item into its correct group. The task includes eco-friendly actions such as: “We have to use energy-
saving bulbs” and “We must plant more trees”, etc. Through this digital matching activity, the students
demonstrate their understanding of environmentally friendly habits and classify examples according
to their correct categories.

Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an activity titled Eco-Friendly Actions using one of the web tools for matching
activities. The teacher also prepares another activity titled Our Environment using one of the interactive
presentation tools (Annex-3). In addition, the teacher develops a third activity named Actions using
one of the web tools for short answer questions. The students are required to bring waste materials
from home to design a recycling product.
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher begins the lesson with the Eco-Friendly Actions activity as a warm-up. The students are
asked to follow the instruction to look at the visuals, click on the pin under each picture, and choose the
sentence that best matches the visual. This interactive activity helps the students recall environmental
vocabulary and actively engage in the lesson.
While Activity
Following the warm-up activity, the teacher continues with the Our Environment activity (Annex-3).
In this stage, the students explore different types of environmental problems, such as air pollution,
water pollution, and waste, through visual materials and short discussions. The teacher encourages
the students to observe the visuals carefully, express what they see, and share their opinions about
the causes and possible solutions to these problems. This activity helps the students develop
environmental awareness and use related vocabulary in meaningful contexts.
After the presentation, the teacher applies the station technique to help the students explore different
environmental problems. Around the classroom, several stations are prepared with titles such as air
pollution, water pollution, deforestation, global warming, and waste. Each station includes a related
picture or short text. The students work in small groups and rotate from one station to another. At each
station, they discuss the problem and write one short solution or suggestion; for example, “We should
plant more trees” for deforestation or “We must stop throwing garbage into rivers” for water pollution.
After visiting all stations, each group shares one idea with the class.
Then, as a follow-up activity connected to each station’s theme, the students use waste materials
they bring from home (plastic bottles, cardboard, paper, tin cans, etc.) to design reuse or recycling
products. For example, at the water pollution station, they can create a water filter model from plastic
bottles; at the deforestation station, mini flower pots from waste bottles; at the air pollution station,
windmills from plastic bottles; at the global warming station, a solar-powered vehicle model; and at
the waste station, a recycling box. Each group then presents its product to the class and shares its
solution idea. This activity sequence promotes collaboration, communication, and critical thinking
skills while allowing students to use environmental vocabulary in context.

Post-Activity
The teacher moves on to the Actions activity as an assessment task. At this stage, the students look
at the picture and use the hint to write the correct word or word group, such as reuse, recycle, or save
energy. This activity helps the teacher assess the students’ understanding of eco-friendly actions and
related vocabulary.
Digital skills, Problem solving
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an activity titled Recycling Challenge using a gamification web tool (Annex-4).
Moreover, the teacher creates another activity titled Environmental Problems using one of the digital
story creation tools (Annex-5). Finally, the teacher develops a third activity titled Our Planet using one
of the web tools for fill-in-the-blank questions (Annex-6).
4. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
At the beginning of the lesson, the teacher conducts the Recycling Challenge as a fun warm-up
activity (Annex-4). In this activity, the students are shown two waste items, such as a plastic bottle,
and a banana peel. The teacher asks questions like “Which bin should this go into?” or “Where does this
item belong?” The students choose the correct recycling bin—plastic, paper, glass, metal, or organic.
This activity helps the students recall what they know about recycling, promotes environmental
awareness, and creates an engaging start to the lesson.
While Activity
After the warm-up activity, the teacher continues with the Environmental Problems digital story
(Annex-5). The students watch a short digital story about a girl who notices pollution in her
neighbourhood and decides to take action to protect nature. The story highlights major environmental
problems such as air pollution, water pollution, and plastic waste. After watching, the teacher asks
comprehension and reflection questions such as: “What problems did the girl see?”, “What actions did
she take?”, and “What can we do to keep our environment clean?”. The students are asked to share their
ideas and examples from daily life, showing awareness of environmental problems and suggesting
simple, eco-friendly solutions.
Post-Activity
The teacher uses the Our Planet activity for assessment (Annex-6). The students are instructed to
read the text carefully and then complete the fill-in-the-blank questions to show their understanding
of the passage. This activity enables the teacher to evaluate the students’ reading comprehension,
vocabulary knowledge, and ability to interpret meaning, rather than simply copying information from
the text.

Homework/Assignment
The teacher assigns the students to create a digital poster on an environmental theme, using one of
the interactive presentation tools. Each student will select one eco-friendly action, for instance saving
water, recycling, planting trees, or reducing plastic use, and design a poster that communicates
the message clearly and engagingly. The poster should include a title that reflects the action (e.g.
Save Water, Save Life), at least three digital visuals or icons that illustrate the steps, and two simple
sentences giving instructions or examples (e.g. Turn off the tap while brushing your teeth. Recycle
plastic bottles.). To make it more impactful, the students are encouraged to add a short slogan that
raises awareness. This assignment not only enables the students to practice vocabulary and writing
skills but also encourages creativity and responsibility for the environment while effectively using
interactive presentation tools.
