Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a game titled Epic Dance Challenge using one of the gamification web tools,
and a visual titled Extreme Sports using one of the visual creation and editing tools (Annex-1). Then,
for the assessment, the teacher prepares a matching activity titled Group the Sports, using one of the
web tools for matching activities.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher commences the lesson by posing the questions, “What is the relation between physical
activity and health?” and “How often do you do sports?”, thereby encouraging the students to reflect on
the importance of physical activity for maintaining good health. Following this discussion, the teacher
introduces the Epic Dance Challenge game. Accompanied by music, the teacher enthusiastically
says, “Let’s dance and move to stay healthy!” and invites the students to stand up. By clicking the
Start Dancing button, the teacher activates the stickman animation, which periodically freezes. The
students are then asked to dance along with the stickman and imitate its pose whenever it freezes.
While Activity
Following the game, the teacher poses questions such as: “What kind of sports are you interested
in?”, “Do you prefer indoor or outdoor sports?”, “Do you prefer team or individual sports?”, “Do you like
taking risks and going beyond your limits, or do you prefer staying in your safe zone?”, “If all sports were
free, which one would you try?”, “If you could create a new sport, what would it look like? How would
people play it?”, and “Imagine you are a coach. How would you encourage lazy people to do more
exercise?”. These questions are designed to activate the students’ prior knowledge, stimulate critical
thinking, and foster personal connections with the topic. By encouraging the students to articulate
their preferences and attitudes toward sports, the teacher not only elicits preliminary information but
also enhances student engagement and prepares the cognitive ground for deeper exploration of
the role of physical activity in health and well-being. The teacher then displays the Extreme Sports
visual, where the students understand short and simple texts to find the main points about adventures
(Annex-1). The students are then invited to form two circles, one inside, the other outside to apply the
fishbowl technique of active learning approach. The inner circle is required to express their preferences
about extreme sports and make comparisons between the extreme sports, and the outer circle asks
the reasons of their preferences. After a set time, the students switch roles, allowing everyone to
participate, which fosters critical thinking, active listening, and structured speaking in an engaging
way. The teacher provides prompt feedback throughout the course of the activity.

Post-Activity
Following the discussion, for the assessment, the teacher initiates the Group the Sports matching
activity, in which the students are required to work in pairs to group the given extreme sports such as
kayaking, rafting, skydiving under the corresponding title indicating the locations where they can be
practised.
Before the Lesson
The teacher procures materials such as rope, netting, and planks, which will later be used in implementing
of the adventure project in the school garden.
2. Ders (Garden)
Pre-Activity
At the beginning of the lesson, the teacher informs the students that they will be in the school garden
for an activity. Once in the garden, the teacher invites the students to form a circle, stands at its centre,
displays the materials, and asks the students to guess the purpose of bringing these materials to the
garden. The teacher evaluates the students’ guesses and indicates which ones are correct or closest to
the intended answer. The teacher asks guiding questions such as “What is the importance of adventure
in your lives?”, “Have you ever done an extreme sport?”, and “What is the relation between extreme
sports and freedom?” These questions encourage the students to discuss the role of freedom and
adventure in their lives.
While Activity
Following the discussion, the teacher emphasises the importance of physical activity and exercise in
our lives and invites the students to initiate an adventure course project in the school garden. The
ensuing details will be elucidated: firstly, the posters indicating the importance of physical activity for
our health, in which all the details of the project will be explained; and secondly, the objective of the
project, which is encouraging the students to socialise and engage in physical activities. The students
are then assigned the task of designing posters for the following lesson, emphasising the importance
of socialising and engaging in physical activities. The posters will be hung in the school corridors and
the trees in the garden to promote the campaign, and the students make announcements to promote
the participation of the volunteer students and the collecting of materials for the adventure area. A
designated adventure course will be established in the garden for the use of all students using the
materials collected. The teacher will act as a guide during the implementation of the project.

Post-Activity
At this stage, the teacher conducts a brainstorming session with the students to reflect on the
proposed project. The students are encouraged to share their observations and discuss the potential
challenges and risks that might arise during the implementation of an adventure course, making
comparisons between different adventure options. To make the process more effective, this stage
can be organised outdoors in groups, which is especially helpful for large classes. The risk and
challenge discussion can also be made role-based to enrich perspectives. For example, the students
can be divided into groups such as the “Safety Team,” the “Organisers,” the “Students’ Club,” and
the “Eco Team.” Each group represents a different viewpoint and contributes by focusing on safety,
organisation, participant experience, and environmental impact. In this way, students collaboratively
identify risks and propose solutions while considering multiple dimensions of the project. Together, they
consider possible solutions and suggest strategies to address these difficulties in advance. Together,
they discuss potential improvements, consider suitable locations in the garden where an adventure
course can be constructed, give reasons, and place the materials to that location. The students will
have the opportunity to use the target language in discussing real-life experiences, thereby fostering
meaningful communication. Moreover, through the adventure course project, the entire school
community will collaboratively engage in the process of designing and creating a tangible product.
This not only promotes cooperative learning and a sense of shared responsibility but also provides
learners with an authentic outcome that reinforces their language use in a purposeful context.
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a Checklist using one of the visual creation and editing tools (Annex-2).
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher shares the Checklist on EBA and divides the class into four groups designated as Adventure
Course, Land Sports, Air Sports, and Water Sports (Annex-2). Thereafter, the teacher prompts the
students to deduce their respective tasks by referring to the checklist in conjunction with the thematic
focus of their assigned group. The teacher then evaluates the process and provides constructive
feedback.
While Activity
The teacher then guides the students to utilise the relevant sections of the checklist, and to allocate
the tasks among themselves. Initially, the Adventure Course group prepares an online survey to be
published on the school website about their favourite extreme sports using one of the survey tools
and analyses the results during the implementation phase of the project. On the school website, the
students announce that volunteers may contribute to the Adventure Course project by providing
materials or by participating in the design and construction of the course. Subsequently, they design
promotional posters for the Adventure Course project, print them, and display them on the school
walls.

Move More, Live More
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
In the final stage, the group undertakes the practical task of setting up the Adventure Course in the
school garden. Meanwhile, the Water Sports, Air Sports, and Land Sports groups employ chatbots to
conduct research on their respective categories and use visual creation and editing tools to create
detailed dissemination materials such as brochures or posters of their respective sports categories.
These dissemination materials are then showcased at stands in the school garden to inform their
peers about the different types of sports. Each group needs to use structures to express preferences,
give reasons for their preferences and make comparisons for their assigned tasks. The students will
contribute to the collective endeavour within the larger group while concurrently engaging in smaller
groups. The teacher provides ongoing feedback to facilitate the students' engagement and progress
throughout the activity.
Post-Activity
The groups share their posters, surveys, and dissemination materials on EBA, and the other group
members may like or comment on them to give feedback to their classmates.
Productivity, Cultural awareness
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a Self-Assessment Form using one of the visual creation and editing tools and
prints it out (Annex-3). The teacher assists the students in printing the posters and dissemination
materials. Furthermore, the students set up three stands in the school garden, each dedicated to one
category: Water Sports, Air Sports, and Land Sports.
4. Ders (Garden)
Pre-Activity
At the beginning of the lesson, the teacher informs the students that they will be in the school garden
for an activity. Once in the garden, the teacher invites the students to form a circle, stands at its centre,
and goes over the responsibilities of each group. The Adventure Course group collects the materials
provided by the school and initiates the design of the course, with opportunities for volunteer students
to participate in the process. Simultaneously, the Water Sports, Air Sports, and Land Sports groups
prepare their stands and begin introducing the extreme sports within their respective categories.
While Activity
Each group carries out the tasks allocated to them, and during the process, the group members are
expected to give detailed explanations of the sports, express their preferences of extreme sports, give
reasons for their preferences using the vocabulary such as challenging, amusing and fascinating
with the objective of practising both the target structures and the lexical items introduced in the unit.
In addition, the students are required to make comparisons between different extreme sports using
the appropriate comparative structures. The Adventure Course group constructs the courses with
the provided materials, and the teacher ensures safety by carrying out detailed checks and provides
feedback to all groups throughout the process.

Post-Activity
The teacher hands out the Self-Assessment Form, and encourages the students to reflect on and

Homework/Assignment
The students are assigned to conduct research on locations in Türkiye where extreme sports can be
practised, and to design a map of Türkiye using visual creation and editing tools to indicate the types
of extreme sports available.
