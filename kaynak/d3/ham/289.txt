Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher is required to design various digital materials to boost motivation and enhance the
student engagement. First, the teacher creates an activity titled Democracy in One Word using one of
tools for creating digital boards or sticky notes and shares the link on EBA. Next, the teacher prepares
a presentation titled A Path to Democracy using one of the interactive presentation tools to introduce
key democracy-related vocabulary, including words such as vote, candidate, ballot box, president,
fairness, rights, and law. A pre-prepared word cloud named Democracy Words is designed using
one of the word cloud creation tools by the teacher. Lastly, for vocabulary reinforcement, the teacher
designs a matching activity, Democracy Match, using a web tool for matching activities (Annex-1).
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher shares the link of the Democracy in One Word with the students and they are asked to
think of words or short phrases related to democracy (e.g. vote, candidate, rights, fairness) and share
their ideas verbally. As the students share their ideas, they actively input them into the digital platform,
which gradually forms a dynamic visual display of the class’s brainstorming and highlights the main
concepts connected to democracy.
While Activity
The teacher introduces the pre-prepared Democracy Words word cloud containing key democracy-
related vocabulary and displays it on the interactive whiteboard. The students are invited to identify the
words they already know and discuss their meanings with the class. This activity serves to activate prior
knowledge, introduce essential vocabulary, and prepare the students for the upcoming presentation.
The teacher presents A Path to Democracy presentation introducing the activity, and asks guiding
questions such as “What happens if a leader is not fair?”, “Do you think voting is the best way to choose
a leader?”, “If you are a candidate, what will you promise to people?” to promote discussion and
comprehension. Prompt feedback is provided continuously to support understanding and engagement
by the teacher.

Post-Activity
For consolidation, the teacher implements the Democracy Match activity focusing on democracy-
related vocabulary, which will help the students consolidate their understanding of the new terms
(Annex-1). The activity is displayed on the interactive whiteboard, and the class is divided into two
groups. The students take turns answering the questions, and each correct response earns points for
their team. The activity is carried out in the form of a quiz competition, which both reinforces key terms
and fosters collaboration, motivation, and engagement. This activity serves as a formative assessment
tool, allowing the teacher to evaluate the students’ understanding of democracy-related vocabulary
and identify words or concepts that may need further clarification in the following lesson.
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares an animation titled The Election Adventure using one of the animation creation
tools, focusing on a school election and the democratic process (Annex-2). The teacher also designs
an online quiz, The Election Adventure Quiz, using one of the web tools for multiple-choice questions
related to the animation, in order to check comprehension and reinforce learning (Annex-3).
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates a “Think-Pair-Share” activity to activate prior knowledge about democracy and
elections. Each student thinks individually about the question: “What makes a fair election?” and writes
2–3 ideas on a small paper. The students are paired and share their ideas with their partner, discussing
similarities and differences. The teacher encourages active participation and provides prompts to help
the students elaborate on their answers.
While Activity
Following the “Think-Pair-Share” activity, the teacher presents the The Election Adventure animation
about a school election and democratic process (Annex-2). Since some words in the story may
be challenging for 6th graders, the teacher pauses the video at key moments to briefly explain or
paraphrase the important terms, using simple language, gestures, and examples on the board. This
way, the students can connect the new words with their meanings. To ensure better comprehension,
the teacher plays the video twice: first, for general understanding, and second, for focusing on specific
elements such as candidates, voting procedures, campaign promises, and fairness in elections.
Immediately after the second viewing, the teacher asks questions such as: “Compare the democratic
decision-making process in the animation with the class president elections in your school. What are
the similarities and differences? How might this event have developed differently in a society that is
not governed democratically?” Then the teacher initiates The Election Adventure Quiz related to the
animation (Annex-3). The students answer interactive questions individually or in pairs, focusing on
comprehension and understanding of democracy-related concepts.

Post-Activity
As a post-activity, the teacher uses Vocabulary Exercise 2: Democracy from EBA, in which the students
are asked to match the given sentence beginnings with the correct word groups to complete the
sentences meaningfully. This activity helps the students reinforce their understanding of democracy-
related vocabulary and sentence structures, while engaging in an interactive matching task. The
teacher integrates this vocabulary exercise with the animation by focusing on key terms such as
candidate, ballot box, rights, and fairness. The students complete the matching task and then explain
one of the completed sentences aloud. This activity both reinforces the link between the animation
and vocabulary practice and serves as an assessment activity enabling the teacher to evaluate the
students’ understanding of democracy-related terms in context.
Productivity
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares for the lesson by selecting suitable digital tools for creating campaign posters
and presentations. The teacher designs a detailed Election Campaign Guideline using one of the
poster and flyer creation tools. The classroom is arranged to support effective group work and
collaboration.
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
In this lesson, the students work in groups to prepare an election campaign by using digital tools. The
teacher explains the task: each group nominates a candidate, prepares campaign promises, a digital
poster, and a presentation about a school election and democratic issue. Roles are assigned within
each group: Candidate, Speech Writer, Digital Designer, Campaign Manager, Supporters. The teacher
demonstrates the digital tools that can be used and provides the Election Campaign Guideline to
support the students in organising their work.
While Activity
The teacher demonstrates how to use digital tools to design campaign posters and presentations.
The students collaborate to create their candidate’s campaign promises, prepare a short campaign
speech, and design a digital poster. During the activity, the teacher circulates around the classroom,
offering guidance, answering questions, and ensuring that all group members actively participate in
their assigned roles.

Post-Activity
Each group presents their campaign to the class. The teacher provides feedback on creativity,
collaboration, and presentation skills. The students reflect on the activity, discussing what they learnt
about democracy and group work.
Homework/Assignment
The students are tasked with writing a short paragraph (6–8 sentences) about “Why democracy is
important in our school and society.” They should include at least five new vocabulary words from the
lesson (e.g. candidate, ballot box, rights, fairness, president). The students will upload their work to EBA
or bring it to the next lesson for sharing.
