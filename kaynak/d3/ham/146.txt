## 5. Sinif/Ingilizce/Family Life - Rhythm of My Family.pdf
SURE:3 KOD:ENG.5.4.L2. ENG.5.4.L3. ENG.5.4.R2. ENG.5.4.R3.
KAV:Family life, Routines and hobbies, Everyday habits, Actions happening at the moment
Learning Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a game titled Routine or Today? using one of the web tools for matching activities
(Annex-1). Next, the teacher prepares an activity titled Who Is Doing What? using one of the web tools for
fill-in-the-blank questions (Annex-2). After that, the teacher creates a video titled A Day of My Family using
one of the video editing tools (Annex-3). For the assessment part, the teacher designs a website titled Let’s
Share Our Routines and Hobbies using one of the web page editing tools (Annex-4).
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the lesson with a warm-up activity to engage all students and create a positive
classroom atmosphere for well-being. The teacher says some short sentences using gestures and facial
expressions, such as “I eat breakfast.” and “I am eating breakfast.” The students decide whether they are
routine or now by raising their hands: one hand for routine and two hands for now. After the activity, the
teacher displays the Routine or Today? digital activity on the interactive whiteboard (Annex-1). The students
complete the task by dragging and dropping each action into the correct category, either a routine or an
action happening now.
While Activity
Following the grouping activity, the teacher proceeds to the Who Is Doing What? activity (Annex-2). In this
activity, learners complete the task through listening only. The students listen to the audio carefully and
match each family member with the correct hobby or activity. After the listening task is completed, the
teacher plays the A Day of My Family video, so that the students can observe the family members and their
daily routines, as well as the activities they are currently engaged in (Annex-3).
Post-Activity
After watching the video, the teacher divides the students into small groups. Each group creates a poster
using one of the visual creation and editing tools, showing a family engaged in different hobbies or daily
routines. The students write a short sentence next to each character to describe what he or she is doing.
After completing their designs, the teacher shares the template link of the Let’s Share Our Routines and
Hobbies site with the students. (Annex-4) Then, the groups upload their work to the site and present it to
their classmates. After the presentations, the teacher gives immediate feedback, praises correct language
use and gently corrects sentences when needed to support ongoing learning.
Classification, Observation-based prediction, Listening/Watching-comprehension,

literacy, Visual literacy, Communication, Cooperation

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates an activity titled Routines and Hobbies Wheel using one of the gamification web tools.
Then, the teacher designs an activity titled Find the Right Person task using one of the web tools for matching
activities.
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The lesson starts with a warm-up game designed to engage all students and boost their motivation. The
teacher says short and simple sentences related to routines and actions happening at the moment. The
students stand up for routine sentences and sit down for actions happening now. The students who make a
mistake are eliminated from the game. On the other hand, the last student who can perform all the actions
correctly wins the game. After the game, the teacher asks simple guiding questions like “What do you do
with your family?” and “What are you doing now at school?”. After this warm-up, the teacher displays the
Routines and Hobbies Wheel on the interactive board and writes the question patterns “What is he/she
doing?” and “What are they doing?” on the board to guide the students. The students come to the board in
pairs. One student spins the wheel and asks the appropriate question and the other student answers. The
teacher models correct expressions when necessary and provides immediate oral feedback.
While Activity
Following the activity, the teacher initiates the Find the Right Person task. In this activity, the students look at
the pictures and match each person with the correct text describing their routine and current activity. Then,
the teacher asks the students to interview their classmates about their routines and what they do on Sundays.
The students use the guiding questions “What do you usually do?” and “What do you do on Sundays?” during
the task. The teacher also introduces some thinking questions such as “Which one do you like more? Why?”,
“Which activity is better for family time?”. Each student interviews at least two peers, selects an additional
question from the list, and takes brief notes while collecting their answers. While the students are working
on the task, the teacher circulates around the classroom and supports the students when needed.
Post-Activity
After interviewing their classmates and taking notes, the students describe their friends’ routines and
activities using short sentences. During the sharing stage, the teacher actively involves the whole class by
asking some follow-up questions such as “Who likes playing games?”, “What do you think about cycling?”,
“Is it good for family time?”. The teacher encourages different students to participate and confirms correct
language use by reformulating sentences when necessary.

Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an activity titled Mysterious Routines and Hobbies using one of the interactive presentation
tools (Annex-5). Following that, the teacher prepares an activity titled Global Routines and Hobbies Explorers
using one of the visual creation and editing tools (Annex-6). For the assessment, the teacher uses the Let’s
Share Our Routines and Hobbies site again (Annex-4).
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the Mysterious Routines and Hobbies activity and asks the students to guess what
James is doing (Annex-5). After collecting the students’ responses, the teacher asks follow-up questions,
such as “Do families in Türkiye do this?”, “Is it important for families?”, “How do you feel?”, and “Does your
family do this activity?”. The students are encouraged to answer these questions with short responses, or
even single words.
While Activity
After guessing the activities and answering the questions, the teacher divides the students into groups and
asks them to conduct some short research into the daily routines and hobbies of people in different countries.
The teacher states that the students can use artificial intelligence powered tools to support their research
process and explains that these tools should only be used for this purpose. The teacher emphasises that
the students are expected to organise the information in their own words, not share any personal data, and
use AI as a guiding tool. The teacher shares the template link of the Global Routines and Hobbies Explorers
with the students and asks them to fill in the blanks based on their findings (Annex-6). While working on
their paper, the students answer guiding questions such as “Which country are you curious about? Why?”,
“What do people in that country do?”,“What are their hobbies?”, “What is similar to your family?”, and “What
is different from your family?”. By answering these questions, they reflect on the similarities and differences
between the daily routines and hobbies of the selected country and their own family.
Post-Activity
After completing the activity, each group uploads their work to the Let’s Share Our Routines and Hobbies site
(Annex-4). Each group then presents their paper to their classmates, sharing what they have learnt about
routines and hobbies around the world. Once a group has finished presenting, the teacher asks the other
groups to evaluate the presentation using the survey tool on the website, by giving points and sharing one
positive comment and one suggestion for improvement.

Homework/Assignment
The students are assigned to create a short video about the routines and hobbies of their family members.
In the video, the students briefly describe what their family members usually do, what they are doing today
or now, and what their hobbies are, using clear and simple sentences. They do not need to show anyone’s
face; instead, they may record related objects, places, or items that represent the routines or hobbies. After
recording and editing their videos, the students upload their work to EBA to share it with their classmates.
This enables them to watch each other’s videos, leave comments and respond to each other’s work in a
supportive way.
Information gathering, Observation-based prediction, Comparison, Reflective thinking,

Cultural literacy, Communication
