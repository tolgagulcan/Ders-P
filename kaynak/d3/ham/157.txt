Learning Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a song titled Our School Life Song-Track using one of the artificial intelligence powered
tools. Then, the teacher selects an appropriate content titled School Life Integrated Skills I from EBA in line
with the lesson outcomes. The teacher prepares a word cloud titled School Life Word Cloud using one of
the word cloud creation tools.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher plays the Our School Life Song-Track and first demonstrates TPR movements for key actions
in the song. For example, the teacher walks in place for ‘walk through the gate’, mimes reading for ‘library’,
pretends eating for ‘canteen’, and lines up for ‘line up’. After modelling each movement, the teacher asks the
students to imitate the actions while listening to the song. The students follow the teacher’s gestures and
repeat the movements throughout the song, helping them understand new vocabulary in an enjoyable,
interactive, and meaningful way. After listening to the song, the teacher asks critical thinking questions to
activate the students’ prior knowledge about school life. For example, the teacher asks, ‘What places do we
usually have in a school?’, ‘Why do we need a library or a science lab?’, and ‘Which school places are your
favourite and why?’.
While Activity
The teacher uses EBA School Life Integrated Skills I interactive module. The students actively participate in
the activities by taking turns, discussing their answers with their classmates, and supporting one another
through peer feedback. First, they click the ‘click the button and watch’ section to view different school
places and then complete a ‘match the words with the pictures’ activity to reinforce vocabulary. Next, they
watch an animation about school clubs and complete drag-and-drop tasks by matching sentences with
the correct club. They also listen to short student statements such as ‘I like planting trees’ and choose the
correct club by clicking the appropriate picture (e.g. Environment Club). Finally, the students watch the
school rules animation by clicking the buttons and learn rules such as ‘Play safe’. Throughout the activity,
the students interact with the digital content step by step, focusing on understanding and matching key
vocabulary with visual and audio support.

Post-Activity
The teacher displays the School Life Word Cloud on the interactive whiteboard for 30 seconds and asks the
students to look carefully and remember as many words as possible. The teacher then hides the word cloud
and divides the class into two or three groups. The groups take turns saying one word they remember. They
have five seconds to respond and cannot repeat a word that has already been given. A different student
answers on each turn, and every correct and clearly pronounced word earns one point. When the groups
cannot remember any more words, the teacher displays the word cloud again. The students identify the
words they missed and pronounce them together. The teacher briefly checks their meanings and provides
feedback when necessary. The group with the highest score wins the game.

Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares an activity titled Sunshine International School using one of the digital story creation
tools (Annex-1). The teacher also designs follow-up questions and interactive quiz items for the post-listening
stage under the title Sunshine School Quiz, including multiple-choice, true-false, and drag-and-drop activities,

2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher uses a short energiser game called ‘Jump to the Word’ to activate the students’ attention and
review key vocabulary from the ‘School Life’ theme. The teacher says different words related and unrelated
to school (e.g. library, canteen, science lab, book, car). When the students hear a school-related word, they
jump once; when they hear a non-related word, they stay still. After a few rounds, a volunteer student is
invited to lead the game by saying the words to the class while their classmates respond with the correct
actions. The activity is fast, fun, and engaging, helping the students focus their attention, revise vocabulary,
and get ready for the writing task in the while stage.

While Activity
To draw the students’ attention to the topic, the teacher could use realia related to school life such as a small
musical instrument for the music club, coloured pencils or paints for the art club, a book for the library, and
simple lab materials like a test tube for the science lab and place them on the table before the listening
activity. Without stopping or interrupting the audio, the teacher briefly points to each item when the related
vocabulary appears in the Sunshine International School listening text, helping the students connect the
words with real objects in a meaningful way (Annex-1). This supports comprehension while keeping the
students focused on listening. After the listening, the teacher places the items on the table and asks simple
questions such as ‘Is this for the library or the canteen?’ or ‘Do we use this in the science lab or at home?’,
encouraging whole-class participation and reinforcing understanding of the main components of the
theme ‘school life.’ The teacher asks the students simple critical thinking questions related to the ‘Sunshine
International School’ listening text to encourage reflection and discussion such as ‘What is school life like
without clubs or rules?’, and ‘Would you like to study at Sunshine International School? Why or why not?’. The
students share their ideas briefly with the class, helping them think about the importance of school places,
rules, and activities while connecting the listening text to their own experiences.
Post-Activity
After listening to the text, the students are ready to move on to the Sunshine School Quiz where they will
answer questions and play interactive games based on the text. The class is divided into two groups, and
the students take part in a game to answer questions from the ‘Sunshine International School’ listening text.
The activity includes multiple choice, true-false, and drag-and-drop question displayed on the interactive
whiteboard. Each group works collaboratively to answer the questions correctly, and they earn points for
each correct response. The group with the highest score at the end of the activity wins the game. This
activity helps the students review the listening content in an engaging, interactive, and motivating way
while reinforcing key vocabulary related to school life.

3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher divides the class into two groups and launches the game Build Your Dream School on the
interactive whiteboard. Each group plays the game separately and tries to complete all three rounds in
the shortest possible time. When the first group is ready, the teacher starts the timer, and the students in
the group take turns selecting a word and placing it under ‘People at School’, ‘Places at School’, or ‘School
Clubs’. The group briefly discusses each word before a different representative uses the board. After all six
words in a round are categorised correctly, a new floor of the school is constructed. The timer stops when
the group completes all 18 words, and its completion time is recorded. The teacher then restarts the game
and follows the same procedure with the second group. The group with the shortest completion time wins.
After both groups have completed the game, the teacher provides brief feedback on the meanings and
pronunciation of the words. The teacher then asks, ‘Which place at school is the most important for learning?
Why?’ and ‘How do school clubs help students learn and work together?’ The groups discuss the questions
briefly and share their ideas with the class.
While Activity
The students complete the guided writing task titled My School Life using a presentation displayed on
the interactive whiteboard. Each slide shows a school role scenario with four options (e.g. coach, student,
secretary, assistant), and the students first choose one role. Then they select a school place (e.g. library,
canteen, conference hall, lab), a school club (e.g. art club, music club), and school rules (e.g. be quiet, be on
time, play safe). After selecting the options, the students use a writing template to create their own short
text. If needed, the students can use the provided structure as support while writing. This activity helps the
students organise ideas and produce simple written sentences about school life in a guided and meaningful
way. Then the students share their written texts with the class by reading them aloud. Volunteers present
their work using the writing template sentences. Starting with the volunteers, the teacher encourages other
students to share their work as well. The teacher and classmates listen and give brief feedback. This activity
helps the students practise speaking and reflect on their writing.
Post-Activity
The teacher uses the Round Robin technique. The students are asked to quickly share their opinions about
school places, clubs, and rules without repeating their friends’ ideas. Each student expresses a short thought
such as which school place they like, which club they want to join, or which rule is important for them. If
a student has no idea, they are passed quickly and the turn moves to the next student. In this way, all the
students participate in a fast and continuous speaking round, which helps them recall vocabulary and
express their ideas orally in a simple and active way.

Homework/Assignment
The students draw their ‘dream school’ on a piece of paper and create a simple poster. They include and label
places such as the library, canteen, science lab, sports field, and club rooms using their imagination. After
completing the poster, they take a photo of their work and upload it to EBA. This task encourages creativity
and helps the students revise school-related vocabulary in a meaningful and visual way.
