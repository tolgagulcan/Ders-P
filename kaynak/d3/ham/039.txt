Activities
Before the Lesson
Before the class, the teacher creates a digital survey on EBA. This survey includes questions such as
“What type of holiday do you prefer?”, “Which activity do you like most?” and “Which place would you
like to visit?” This process facilitates the discovery of shared preferences and possible holiday concepts
through data analysis. The teacher prepares a Holiday Types presentation including various holiday
types by using one of the interactive presentation tools.
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher presents slides showcasing different types of holidays (beach vacations, city breaks,
adventure trips, etc.) to prepare the students for upcoming activities, the Holiday Types presentation.
While Activity
The teacher asks the students to brainstorm ideas for the next holiday, providing them with clues and
guiding them through the process: “Suppose that you are going on a holiday with your family. What kind
of holiday do you prefer? Why?”
The students are divided into groups to discuss their preferred holiday types within the allotted time set
by the teacher. Groups with similar preferences are formed. The teacher provides guidance as needed.
Subsequently, each group submits a written summary outlining their preferred holiday destination and
the reasons behind their choice.
The teacher asks the groups to turn their reports into presentations. For this, the teacher asks the students
to use interactive presentation tools. The teacher then poses a new question for group discussion: “As a
group, discuss your individual preferences and try to come up with a new holiday idea that combines
elements everyone would enjoy. Where would you go, what would you do, and why would this be a great
holiday for your combined group?” The teacher tells the students to be ready to make these presentations
in the second lesson.

Post-Activity
The students are required to create interactive vacation presentations using digital tools. To enhance
engagement, they must incorporate images, videos, and audio. The teacher offers guidance on effective

Before the Lesson
The teacher searches online map applications with various tourist destinations and prepares three sets
of coloured cards (Green: Excellent; Blue: Acceptable; Red: Needs Improvement). Then, an observation
form with evaluation criteria is created by the teacher and it is stated that the students will be assessed
accordingly (Annex-1).
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher and the students choose one of the online map applications to see different tourist
destinations around the world. The teacher asks the students to share if they recognise any of the
places. With this activity, the teacher arouses curiosity about the lesson and attracts the attention of
the students.
While Activity
The teacher distributes three cards to the students and explains how to use them (e.g., Listen carefully
to your friends’ presentations. Evaluate your friends using the cards given to you at the end of the
presentation). The teacher introduces the observation form (Annex-1). The teacher informs the students
in advance about the criteria on the form and provides detailed explanations of these criteria. One
student from each group presents their group’s ideal vacation. The presentation guidelines emphasise
correct grammar and vocabulary.
Each group presents their work, followed by peer evaluation using provided cards. The teacher observes
this process, offering guidance as needed.
Post-Activity
The teacher requires the students to create digital reflective journal using web tools for self assessment.
In these journals, the students reflect on their experience during the lessons, what they learned, and
areas for improvement. The teacher reviews these journals and provides personalised feedback to
each student.

Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares a digital board titled “Vacation Dreams” on EBA, creates a “word box” for the

3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher creates a digital board titled “Vacation Dreams” and uses EBA for this activity. The students
brainstorm and post images, short videos, or text representing their ideal vacation destinations.
While Activity
The teacher enhances the students’ vocabulary acquisition through an engaging competition. The
class is divided into two groups. The teacher prepares ten vocabulary words related to the unit (e.g.
ancient, rural, landmark etc.), folds them, and places them in a box labelled “word box”. During the
competition, the students from each group take turns drawing a word from the box. If a student can
correctly define the word, their group gets a point. If the student is unable to define the word, it will be
returned to the box. The game continues until one group manages to score the most points.
Additionally, to reinforce writing skills, the students access Writing Exercise 1: Tourism on EBA. The teacher
reads the writing text and puts the emphasis on holiday activities and places. They then use this outline
to compose a paragraph about a place they have visited, integrating vocabulary from the competition.
Post-Activity
The teacher displays sample brochures by using one of the interactive presentation tools. The students
are then required to create brochures based on their outline and the teacher provides support when
needed. By integrating vocabulary reinforcement through competition with writing exercises and
brochure design, the students engage actively in both language acquisition and digital literacy skills
development.

While Activity
The teacher initiates Listening Exercise: Tourism on EBA to reinforce the vocabulary and structures
related to unit outcomes. The students listen to the text and answer questions about the listening text
while improving their listening skills.
To encourage vocabulary acquisition, a word association test is employed (Annex-2). This activity
can be conducted individually or in groups. The students are briefed on the test procedure and the
associated marking scheme. They are asked to senerate words and phrases associated with “tourism
and preferences” and subsequently incorporate these into sentences that relate to “holiday plans”.
One mark is awarded for each valid word or phrase linked to “holiday plans” (e.g. seaside, abroad,
souvenir). Two marks are awarded for each meaningful sentence pertaining to “holiday plans” (e.g. “I
prefer urban places; I think they are fascinating.”, “I’d rather have a cultural holiday than a historical
one.”, “I prefer a historical holiday.”).
Post-Activity
The teacher reviews and evaluates the students’ responses, providing feedback based on their
performance.
