Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher invites the students to choose a sport, dress in suitable attire, and bring any relevant
equipment. In the absence of real equipment, the students may use toy or cardboard versions. These
materials and visual representations will be used in an upcoming class presentation. Prior to the lesson,
the teacher obtains parental permission for the photography activity. The teacher prepares a student
assessment form based on an analytical rubric (Annex-1).
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher enters the classroom holding a ball and explains that the class is going to play a game.
The students then pass the ball around each saying a word related to a chosen sport. Each student
should repeat the words articulated in advance; otherwise, they must perform a sporting activity (e.g. a
tennis stroke) that has not been mentioned before, after which the next student will guess the sport. The
activity continues until a sufficient amount of time has passed, energising the students for the lesson.
While Activity
The teacher prompts the students to guess the sport based on the materials they have brought to class.
Following this, the teacher asks the students to pose with their equipment to simulate the sport, and
takes the students’ photos in action. If sufficient tablets are available, the students may take their own
photos and send them to the teacher. If the school provides access to various sports fields, groups can
also visit these areas to take relevant photos. Time should be allocated for this, with clear instructions.
If the activity remains in the classroom, during the photo session, both the teacher and the students
verbally identify the sport being demonstrated. For example, the teacher might ask, “What is the name
of this sport?”, to which the students would respond, “This sport is football.” The teacher can then reinforce
the vocabulary and context by adding, for example, “He is playing football and kicking the ball now.”
Post-Activity
The teacher stores the photos on the computer and forms groups of three or four students using a
classroom management tool. The teacher then guides the groups in creating online vocabulary cards
or flashcards using a selected visual creation and editing tool. The teacher demonstrates how to use
the tool and provides clear instructions. The students then gather at the computers and follow the
teacher’s guidance to complete the task.

For the cards, the students launch the visual creation and editing tool to upload the first photo, and

Once the cards are completed, the students present them by practising the pronunciation of the sports

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a matching activity titled Group the Sports using one of the gamified measurement
and evaluation web tools (Annex-2).
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher informs the class that they are going to play a game and asks for a student volunteer.
The volunteer leaves the room while the rest of the class selects a sport for them to act out. Once the
volunteer returns, the other students act out the chosen sport, and the volunteer has to guess what it is.
The game then continues with different students taking it in turns to be a volunteer. Once several rounds
have been completed, the warm-up activity concludes.
While Activity
The teacher uses a classroom management tool to display the groups and allows the tool to randomly
select one group. The vocabulary cards created by the chosen group are then printed and distributed to
all the students. The teacher advises the students to cut out the cards and use them as study materials
at home.
The teacher introduces the idea that sports can be categorised under specific headings and explains
the concept. For example, some sports are team activities, while others are individual. The teacher also
highlights that certain sports are played indoors, while others are better suited to outdoor environments,
and provides relevant examples. The students are encouraged to contribute their own examples to
foster engagement in the discussion. As the teacher explains, the students take notes to reinforce their
understanding of the classifications.

Post-Activity
The teacher initiates the next part of the lesson by launching the Group the Sports matching activity
(Annex-2). This activity contains a list of sports, categorised as team or individual, and indoor or outdoor.
One by one, the students come to the board, select a sport from the list, and identify the correct
category by matching it accordingly. Each student takes their turn completing the activity, reinforcing
their understanding of how different sports are classified through shared whole class participation. As
this is a drag-and-drop activity, it should be carried out in the classroom environment with the teacher
, who can then check the answers and provide feedback to the students. In order to reinforce the topic,
the class completes, Vocabulary Exercise 1: Sports on EBA.
Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to bring any toys that they have. They also provide colourful paper and
crayons as the students are going to design a scene. The teacher uploads the students’ names to one
of the classroom management tools. For each group in the lesson, the teacher is required to provide as
many tablets as possible.
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
An engaging activity titled “Tide’s In/Tide’s Out” is set to commence the session. In preparation for this
game, the teacher draws a line, representing a beach, and asks the students to stand behind it. The
rules are simple: When the teacher announces, “Tide’s out!”, the students are to jump forward, and when
the teacher calls “Tide’s in!”, they must jump backwards. If “Tide’s out!” is called twice consecutively and
a student makes an incorrect move, that student is eliminated from the game. The aim of the activity
is to encourage the students to be both quick and attentive to the instructions. Upon completion, the
students are expected to be energised, focused, and ready to begin the lesson, having engaged in a
brief but enjoyable physical exercise.
While Activity
The teacher launches the classroom management tool and organises the students into groups. The
teacher introduces a project in which the students write a story about sports, sports equipment or a
significant moment in a game. The students then transfer their story into a visual format by designing
an outline on cardboard. A group writing about a football match might represent a scene with a green
cardboard field and a goal. The students then act out the story using toys from home. They then upload
the photographs to a video editing tool to create a video.
The teacher utilises a classroom management tool to organise the students into groups for collaborative
work. Each group is given a short amount of time to choose a name for their group and a sport for their
story. The teacher documents the selections before offering further guidance. The students are given
structure guidelines, covering the number of sentences and the elements to include. The students then
write their stories on tablets or computers, asking the teacher for help and using online dictionaries.

Post-Activity
Once they have finalised their stories, the students create visual and performative representations.
Then, they design the stage. They create visual representations using provided materials and toys to
act out their stories. The teacher circulates around the classroom, offering guidance and assistance
as needed. For the students who are unable to complete their set designs within the current session,
additional time will be provided during the next lesson. This ensures all groups can fully develop their
creative scenes before presenting and acting out their stories.
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares a group assessment form to evaluate the collaborative efforts of each group
(Annex-3). Additionally, a Self-Assessment Rubric is created using a web tool for self-assessment
(Annex-4). These forms assess various aspects of the project, such as task completion, grammatical
accuracy, teamwork, and problem-solving skills, amongst others.
4. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces a sports-themed version of “Simon Says” to reinforce vocabulary. The rules are
simple. The students only follow commands starting with “Simon says.” If they follow a command that
does not start with this phrase, they are eliminated.
The teacher begins with simple instructions such as “Simon says clap your hands” and gradually
increases the pace. To tie it into the sports unit, the teacher uses prompts such as “Simon says play
football” or “Simon says hit the ball with a racket.” This fun and active game helps the students practise,
consolidate sports-related vocabulary through repetition and movement.
While Activity
In this session, the students create stop-motion videos based on the stories, texts and scenes that
they developed previously. The teacher introduces stop-motion as a video editing tool, explaining
that it involves taking pictures of static 3D objects, such as toys, to make them appear to move. The
teacher explains the process positioning the toys, taking a photo after each small movement, and then
uploading the photos to a video editing tool to create the video. The students choose toys and objects
to illustrate their stories, e.g. a football match or an athlete’s routine. They then take photos of each toy
as it moves. The teacher shows how to upload these photos to a video editing tool in order to create a
smooth video. The teacher provides examples to spark creativity, such as a ball slowly moving towards
a goal and scoring, with a narration describing the action. Another example is showing an athlete’s
daily routine, with each action represented by a toy or object. The students are then required to add
subtitles to the video by writing out their stories. The students can read their texts aloud and record a
voice-over for the video if time allows. This process combines writing, design, photography and digital
skills, and enables the students to learn language related to sports and storytelling.

Post-Activity
Once the students have finished making their stop-motion videos, the teacher plays them on the
interactive whiteboard for the whole class to watch. This viewing session allows the students to
appreciate each other’s creativity and recognise the different approaches taken in the project.
Afterwards, the teacher encourages the students to complete the group assessment form and the
Self-Assessment Rubric (Annex-3 and Annex-4). The teacher clarifies that the students will assess both
their own performance and that of their group members. These evaluations will cover aspects such as
participation, collaboration, and contribution to the final product. At the end of the session, the teacher
encourages the students to upload their videos to EBA. By sharing their projects on this platform, the
students have the opportunity to watch their videos again, review their peers’ work, and showcase their
efforts to a wider audience. This final step supports ongoing engagement and fosters pride in their
completed projects beyond the classroom.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher uses an artificial intelligence powered tool to create quizzes based on student videos and
stories focusing on the subject of sports and equipment. After reviewing the questions, the teacher
uploads them to a gamified measurement and evaluation web tool, turning the quiz into an interactive
experience. The students access the quiz via a shared link, which enables them to demonstrate their
understanding of their own videos and those of their peers.
The teacher assigns Grammar Exercise 1: Sports and Grammar Exercise 4: Sports activities to the
students through EBA and then assesses their performance.
