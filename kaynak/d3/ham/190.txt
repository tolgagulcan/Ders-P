Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher shares Dreams video with the students via EBA before the first lesson. The students are
asked to watch the video and take notes. The teacher prepares cards with different predictions in the
future tense.
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher divides the class into teams to check the students’ understanding of the future tense and
activate prior knowledge of making predictions. The teacher prepares cards with different predictions
in the future tense. The first team draws a card and reads the prediction. The other teams decide
whether the prediction is probable or improbable. If they guess correctly, they get a point. After each
round, discuss the prediction and the future tense used. This activity helps the students review future
tense concepts and activate prior knowledge about making predictions.
While Activity
In order to introduce the concept of predictions, the teacher first explains that these are guesses about
what might happen in the future based on past experiences, current information, and sometimes
intuition. The teacher then initiates an artificial powered virtual assistant on the interactive whiteboard
and demonstrates how to use a virtual assistant to make predictions, asking the virtual assistant
questions such as “What will the weather be like tomorrow?” and “What's the next holiday?” The students
listen to the responses and are encouraged to note any new vocabulary related to predictions.

Following the demonstration, the students are divided into pairs or small groups. Before asking
questions, the teacher instructs them to introduce themselves to the virtual assistant. Each group
should then ask their own questions about various future scenarios. For instance, they might ask,
“Will I be successful in my career?” and “Will I make good friends?” The virtual assistant’s responses
are designed to encourage self-actualisation and the cultivation of positive relationships and may
include predictions such as “You will be successful in your career. Definitely! Also, you will be very
happy in the future. You will make good friends.” The students are encouraged to take note of any new
vocabulary related to predictions as they listen to the responses.
Post-Activity
To conclude the lesson, the teacher reviews the key vocabulary and concepts with the students. The
students share their own predictions for the future, using phrases like “I hope so.”, “I may” and “probably”
and integrate the vocabulary they have learned. For example, the students might discuss their dreams
by saying, “I will definitely go to university, but I may not live in a big city.” The teacher encourages the
students to express their expectations and hopes for the future, reinforcing the lesson’s objectives.
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares visuals related to future and dreams. The teacher selects a suitable animation
creation tool for the lesson.
2. Ders ( IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The lesson begins with a visual warmer where the students identify images related to future dreams
(e.g. a car, a house, graduation, marriage). The teacher then encourages the students to discuss the
images and their potential meanings, fostering a deeper connection to the theme. By asking open-
ended questions and facilitating a discussion, the teacher creates a stimulating and engaging
learning environment that encourages the students to reflect on their own aspirations and connect
them to the images presented.
While Activity
The teacher introduces an animation creation tool to the class and gives the instruction for the new
activity: “Now, dream that the year is 2050. How old will you be? Where will you be? What will you do
and have? Think about these questions and make your animation.” The students then brainstorm
their future visions and create their own animated stories using the tool. They share their creations
with the class and discuss their thoughts about the future, reflecting on their personal goals and
aspirations. Throughout the process, the teacher provides guidance and feedback to support the
students' learning.
Post-Activity
Following the creation of their animations, the students will be required to provide a concise reflection
on their experience. They will then discuss their thoughts with their classmates in small groups and
the whole class. This process will facilitate a more profound examination of the knowledge they have
acquired and its potential applications in future contexts.

Homework/Assignment
The teacher assigns Grammar Exercise 2: Dreams and Vocabulary Exercise 2: Dreams on EBA. The

Before the Lesson
The guideline for the warm-up exercise is prepared by the teacher (Annex-1). The teacher chooses
one of the interactive presentation web tools and introduces the tool to the students. The students are
informed that their performance will be evaluated by the teacher at the end of the lesson using an
assessment form (Annex-2).
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher asks the students to draw their left or right hand on a piece of white cardboard. The teacher
writes the prompts on the board (Annex-1). The students write their answers neatly on each finger of
their drawn hand. The teacher encourages the students to share their designs and predictions with
the class and facilitates a discussion about the various predictions and the future tense used in each
one.
While Activity
The objective of this lesson is to utilise the future tense to predict the future world. The students
commence by brainstorming their personal predictions for the future and reviewing future tense
concepts. The teacher then provides a detailed explanation of how to predict the future, emphasising
the importance of researching current trends and making educated guesses about how they might
evolve. The students are then divided into groups and assigned research topics related to future
trends, such as technology, the environment or society. The teacher provides them with resources and
guidelines for conducting this research. The students then conduct thorough research using reliable
sources and meticulously record their findings.
Post-Activity
The students are expected to create presentations that summarise their research and articulate their
predictions, using persuasive language and evidence to support their arguments. They are required
to use one of the interactive presentation tools to prepare their presentations and then engage in
a comprehensive discussion of the various predictions, considering their potential advantages,
disadvantages and influencing factors. This lesson incorporates research, presentation skills, critical
thinking and persuasive writing, while reinforcing the concept of the future tense. The teacher then
assesses the students' presentations by filling in the student assessment form (Annex-2).

Before the Lesson
The teacher selects a digital publishing tool to create a digital magazine and introduces the tool to

4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
In order to evaluate the students’ comprehension of the future tense and to encourage their ability to
make predictions in a visual format, the teacher creates a timeline on the board or distributes a sheet
of paper to each student. The timeline is then labelled with time periods, such as “1 year from now”, “5
years from now” and “10 years from now”. The students are then instructed to write their predictions
for the future on the timeline, employing future tense verbs. The students then share their predictions
with the class, facilitating a discussion about the varied predictions and the future tense employed in
each. This activity has been designed to assist the students in developing a visual understanding of
future time and to encourage the creative application of future tense.
While Activity
The teacher introduces a digital publishing tool to create a digital magazine and demonstrates its
features. The students then brainstorm ideas for their future lives and write about them using future
tense. They can create their digital magazines, adding text, images, and other elements. The teacher
asks the students to initiate the tool to create their digital magazines and write about their lives in
2050. The students imagine their future selves and write about their aspirations, goals, and dreams.
For instance, “I will be a teacher”, “I will have a car and a house”, “I will be rich”, “I will run a company”, etc.
After they write their future story, they complete their future book. The title of this book may be chosen
by the students, for example, “Me in 2050, Me in the Future”.
Post-Activity
The students are required to upload links of their digital magazines to EBA and provide comments on
each other’s work. This activity significantly enhances the students’ writing, design, and digital skills,
while also fostering their creativity and imagination.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher assigns the students to complete Writing Exercise 1: Tom, Jenny and Me! and Writing
Exercise 2-My New Friend on EBA.
