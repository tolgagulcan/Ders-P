Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates an animation named Fun Planet Facts, using one of the animation creation tools
(Annex-1). Then, the teacher prepares a video titled Journey Through the Mysterious Universe using
one of the video editing tools (Annex-2). Lastly, for the assessment, the teacher prepares an activity
named Fill-in-the-Blanks with Comparatives using one of the web tools for fill-in-the-blank questions
(Annex-3).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher aims to conduct an integrated STEM English lesson that connects science and language
through the themes of space and exploration. To spark curiosity and imagination, the teacher initiates
the lesson by asking questions such as “If you could visit any planet, which one would you choose
and why?”, “Do you think there could be life on another planet?”, and “What do you think lies beyond
the stars we can see?”. These questions are designed to stimulate creative thinking, preparing the
students for the theme of exploration. Following the questions, the teacher initiates the Fun Planet
Facts animation, where the students identify specific information about general truths about the
planets and comparatives (Annex-1).
While Activity
Following the animation, the teacher asks questions such as “What do you think about the mysteries
of the universe?”, “What is the most interesting thing about the universe?” to promote higher-order
thinking and guide the students towards reflective observation. Through these questions, learners
are encouraged to make personal interpretations, connect visual input with prior knowledge, and
express curiosity-driven ideas in English. The teacher says “Let’s continue to discover the mysteries
of the universe.”, and initiates the Journey Through the Mysterious Universe video, through which
the students can recognise additional scientific facts and mysteries presented in simple oral texts
(Annex-2). Upon completion of the video, the teacher forms groups of four to encourage the students
to work collaboratively. The groups are invited to work in their groups to discuss the concepts of black
holes and comets, encouraging them to share their ideas, make comparisons, and draw simple
conclusions based on the information they have observed. The teacher may ask some questions
such as “What might happen if a comet came very close to Earth?”, “How would you feel if you could
observe a black hole from a safe distance?” to develop higher-order thinking skills. Through this
collaborative discussion, the students not only enhance their communicative competence but also
cultivate critical thinking, creativity, and scientific reasoning, thereby aligning the activity with the
interdisciplinary objectives of STEM education.

Post-Activity
Following the discussion, the teacher displays the Fill-in-the-Blanks with Comparatives activity and

Active listening, Digital literacy
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a game titled Guess the Image using one of the gamification web tools. The
students are required to bring Styrofoam balls, paints, paintbrushes, wooden sticks, cardboard bases,
glue and scissors to design the Solar System.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher shares the Guess the Image game with the students, where the students slide the circle to
discover the hidden images, such as a satellite and a space shuttle, and write the word for that image.
The student who has the most correct answers or finishes first will be declared the winner. The teacher
uses this activity to engage the students in a fun and interactive warm-up activity that activates their
prior knowledge and recalls key vocabulary related to the unit.
While Activity
Following the vocabulary activation stage, the teacher asks the students to work in groups of six. Within
each group, half of the students are responsible for creating a digital model of the Solar System using
one of the artificial intelligence-powered tools, while the other half design a physical model based on the
same data. Within each group, basic roles such as data researchers, engineers/builders, presenters and
coordinators will be assigned to improve the implementation of the course. The students researching
the planets collect information about their diameter, average temperature, and distance from the Sun.
After gathering the necessary data, each group integrates their findings into a short presentation using
an interactive presentation tool. During this process, the students are encouraged to use comparative
adjectives to describe the planets, reinforcing the target language structure in a meaningful, content-
based context. Additionally, they may include interesting facts about the universe or the Solar System in
their presentations. In this STEM-integrated process, the students develop interdisciplinary connections
across science, maths, technology, engineering, and English through the design of both digital and
physical models. The teacher provides prompt feedback throughout the activity.

Post-Activity
The students present both their digital presentations and the physical models they have created to
their classmates. After all presentations are completed, they organise a “Space Exhibition” in their
classroom to display all the models. Their peers provide constructive feedback on their work.
Productivity, Digital literacy
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a matching activity titled Match the Verbs using one of the web tools for matching
activities. Then the teacher designs a story named Lost in Space using one of the digital story creation
tools (Annex-4). Lastly, for the assessment, the teacher prepares an activity titled Answer the Questions
using one of the web tools for multiple-choice questions (Annex-5).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the Match the Verbs activity, in which the students match the verbs, such as go
and make, with their past forms. This serves as a warm-up activity to help them recall verb forms
before reading the story about space travel.
While Activity
Following the matching activity, the teacher asks questions such as “Who was the first person to travel
to space?” and “Do you know the name of the first animal that travelled to space?”. These questions
activate the students’ background knowledge and arouse their curiosity about the topic. After eliciting
a few answers, the teacher briefly introduces Yuri Gagarin and Laika, showing their pictures to help
the students make connections with the upcoming story. The teacher briefly tells Laika’s story: “Laika
was a small, stray dog from Moscow, Russia. In 1957, she became the first living creature to travel into
space. On November 3, 1957, Laika was sent into space on the Sputnik 2 spacecraft. Unfortunately, the
technology at that time could not bring her back to Earth safely.”, and initiates the Lost in Space story,
in which the students read about the adventure of astronaut Leo and a cat named Cutie (Annex-4).
Upon completing the story, the teacher asks, “If you were Leo, how would you feel and what would
you do?”. The students may be asked to write a few sentences expressing their feelings as if they were
Leo or Cutie. The aim of this stage is to encourage the students to think critically, empathise with the
characters, and express their ideas using the target language.

Post-Activity
Following the reading, to assess the students’ comprehension, the teacher displays the Answer the
Questions multiple-choice questions and asks the students to work in pairs to answer the questions
(Annex-5).
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher invites the students to work in groups of four to create a word cloud using one of the word
cloud creation tools. The word cloud may consist of the words related to science, space, planets, and
space travels. The students are required to form sentences using the words in their word cloud. This
activity combines creativity, vocabulary building, and sentence structure practice.
While Activity
Following the creation of the word cloud, the teacher shares the link of the Lost in Space story with
the students and asks them to write the rest of the story (Annex-4). The students continue to work in
the same group, brainstorm about the alternative endings and write an interesting, surprising end
using the simple past tense. The teacher aims to develop the students’ creative writing and digital
skills, while reinforcing their understanding and use of the simple past tense through meaningful story
completion tasks. The teacher provides prompt feedback throughout the activity.
Post-Activity
The groups share their stories on EBA. The other groups read the stories, comment, or like the alternative
endings to give feedback.
Homework/Assignment
The students are assigned to complete the Reading Exercise: Planets on EBA. The students are required
to read the passage and answer the questions.
