Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares a number of slips of paper, each bearing the name of a building, such as a post
office, pharmacy, bakery, museum, shopping mall, library, and toy store, for use in the Chinese whisper
game. The teacher also prepares an Exploring Italy with Anna activity using an animation creation tool
(Annex 1). Following the animation, the teacher prepares a Building Match-Up using one of the web tools
for matching activity (Annex-2). For the last activity, the teacher uses a gamification web tool to create
a Guess the Place! activity (Annex-3).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
To initiate the activity, the teacher organises the students into small, diverse groups of five to six to foster
peer support and collaboration. The teacher then introduces the “Chinese Whispers”, a game that lets
the students practise vocabulary related to buildings. One student from each team is invited to the
front to read a slip with a building name on it. The phrase is then passed along in a whisper from one
group member to the next until the final participant reveals what they have heard. This game enhances
listening skills, reinforces vocabulary, and fosters teamwork.

While Activity
Following the game, the students participate in the Exploring Italy with Anna activity, where they watch
an engaging animation featuring buildings in the city (Annex-1). As they follow along, they identify
prepositions (e.g. “The bank is next to the post office.”) and name of the buildings. This interactive
experience enhances their comprehension skills related to buildings and prepositions.
Following the animation, in the Building Match-Up activity, the students listen to the audio files and
match them with the corresponding pictures of buildings, reinforcing their understanding of the sounds
associated with various places (Annex-2).
Post-Activity
Following the previous activity, the students engage in the Guess the Place! activity where they look at
a picture and guess the place depicted by stating letters (Annex-3). This prompts them to examine the
image closely. Both activities integrate a visual matching component to enhance the learning process,
ensuring an enjoyable and effective experience for the students as they expand their vocabulary and
comprehension of buildings and locations.
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs The Ball of Max activity using an animation creation tool (Annex-4). Then the teacher
prepares a Let’s Match the Prepositions activity using a web tool for matching activities (Annex-5).
2. Ders ( IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces the “Simon Says” game, the students engage in a fun and active way to learn
prepositions. The teacher provides clear and encouraging instructions for the students to follow such
as “Simon says put your pencil on your desk.”, “Simon says place your notebook under your chair.” or
“Simon says hold your book next to your backpack.” When the teacher doesn’t say “Simon says...” the
students must remain still. This interactive game encourages movement and helps students understand
locations through simple actions, making the lesson both enjoyable and educational.
While Activity
After the game, the teacher introduces an animated story titled The Ball of Max (Annex-4). In this activity,
the students are required to read and engage with the story, which revolves around Max and his ball. The
animation visually introduces key prepositions such as “in”, “on” ,“under”, and “next to” through engaging
scenes, helping the students grasp these concepts in a fun and interactive way.

Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
Following the animated story, the students participate in the Let’s Match the Prepositions activity
(Annex-5). In this activity, the students are required to listen to audio clips that describe different
prepositions and match them with the corresponding pictures. The interactive format reinforces their
understanding of prepositions by connecting spoken language with visual cues. This activity promotes
listening comprehension and awareness of locations, making it an engaging and effective way to
practice prepositions.
Post-Activity
After completing the matching activity, the students transition to create a “Prepositions Poster” with
a digital poster and flyer creation tool. Working in groups, they design posters featuring images and
sentences (e.g. “The toy is under the chair.”) based on a theme of their choice. This activity allows
the students to creatively showcase their understanding of prepositions and practice using them in
context. Then they present their posters to the class, reinforcing their understanding of prepositions
while fostering creativity, teamwork, and digital skills.
Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to complete Vocabulary Exercise 1: My Town from EBA prior to the lesson.
The teacher prepares a Where Is Bunny? animation using an animation creation tool (Annex-6). Following
this, the teacher creates a Where Is the Bird? interactive activity with a web tool for matching activities
(Annex-7). The final activity, Place It Right! also created with a web tool for matching activities, involves
additional matching exercises (Annex-8). Additionally, the teacher assigns a Daily Reflection Paper
created using a poster and flyer creation tool, for the students to complete as homework (Annex-9).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher brings a small toy rabbit to engage the students in an interactive learning activity focused
on practicing prepositions through a hide-and-seek game. The teacher hides the rabbit in various
locations around the classroom, such as under a chair or behind a desk, etc. The students take turns
asking yes/no questions to determine the rabbit’s location. The student who successfully finds the rabbit
gets the chance to hide it in the next round. This activity not only reinforces prepositional vocabulary
but also encourages collaboration and active participation amongst the students.

While Activity
After the game, the teacher presents the Where Is Bunny? animation, featuring the character Bunny, to
engage the students in a storytelling activity (Annex-6). During the animation, the teacher encourages
the students to read along, enhancing their comprehension and vocabulary. This activity smoothly
transitions from the previous game, reinforcing the use of prepositions in a fun and interactive way.
Together, these activities encourage active participation and critical thinking, while deepening the
students’ understanding of the story’s themes. After reading the story, the teacher guides the students to
the Where Is the Bird? interactive activity, where they examine pictures and click on circles to mark their
answers (Annex-7). The students are required to choose the correct preposition that corresponds to the
images. This interactive element creates a dynamic learning experience, enhancing comprehension
and encouraging active participation.
Post-Activity
After completing the interactive activity, the teacher moves on to the final task. In the Place It Right!
activity, the students are required to examine the image and drag the appropriate preposition into the
correct position (Annex-8). This serves as a practical assessment of their understanding, reinforces the
prepositions in an engaging and interactive way, and informs the teacher about their progress.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher assigns the Daily Reflection Paper, in which the students reflect on their feelings about the
lesson, identify the activity they found most challenging and explain why, and share which activity they
enjoyed the most and why (Annex-9). They are also encouraged to consider what they would like to do
in the next lesson. The teacher then provides commentary and feedback. As a follow-up assignment,
the students are expected to demonstrate their understanding of prepositions by creatively illustrating
real-life situations using a toy, short film, or animation that includes at least five prepositions.
