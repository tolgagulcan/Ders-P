Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to complete Vocabulary Exercise 1: Wild Animals on EBA. In this activity,
the students match words related to wild animals and their habitats with images that represent those
words. The teacher then designs a gamified activity titled Animals using one of the gamification
web tools. The teacher also creates a presentation called Characteristics of Animals using one of
the interactive presentation tools. Additionally, Animals Group and Animal Explorers activities are
prepared using web tools for matching activities (Annex-1).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher employs the Animals anagram activity as a warm-up, in which the students drag letters
into the correct positions to unscramble an animal-related words or phrases such as ‘turtle’ or
‘owl’. This interactive and fun activity helps students engage with the lesson while improving their
vocabulary, spelling and problem-solving skills.
While Activity
Following the warm-up activity, the teacher moves on to the Animal Explorers activity. In this task, the
students are required to listen to the audio clips and match each word or phrase such as ‘caterpillar’
or ‘monkey’, with the corresponding image. After the matching activity, the teacher continues with
the Characteristics of Animals presentation. During this activity, the students carefully review the
presentation and answer questions about its content. To enhance the effectiveness of the process,
the teacher pauses the video at specific points for analysis, enabling the students to develop a deeper
understanding of the material. This approach helps students to process information, grasp the key
concepts of the presentation and develop their critical thinking skills.
Post-Activity
For the assessment, the teacher invites the students to complete the Animals Group activity, in
which they categorise animals into groups such as mammals, reptiles, insects, and birds (Annex-1).
The students drag and drop each animal into its correct group, which helps them practise their
classification skills and reinforce their understanding of the different types of animals.

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a crossword named Animal World and a word search exercise named Body Parts
using one of the gamification web tools. The teacher also designs a digital story titled The Big Elephant
and the Little Panda using one of the digital story creation tools (Annex-2). Additionally, the teacher
prepares an Animal ID exercise using one of the web tools for fill-in-the-blank questions (Annex-3).
The teacher divides the students into groups by having them count off in sequence (e.g. “1, 2, 3, 4” for
four groups). All the students who say “1” form Group 1, those who say “2” form Group 2, and so on. This
method ensures even distribution and is quick to implement.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
As a warm-up activity, the teacher presents the Animal World crossword exercise, in which the students
are encouraged to select a word and write it based on the corresponding image. This activity helps
the students to connect visual cues with vocabulary, reinforcing their spelling, word recognition, and
understanding of animal-related terms in an interactive way.
While Activity
Following the crossword activity, the teacher continues with The Big Elephant and the Little Panda
digital story (Annex-2). While the students listen to the story, the teacher emphasises the body parts
of the animals mentioned. This strategy promotes vocabulary acquisition by drawing the students’
attention to key words within the context of the story, thereby fostering a more engaging and interactive
learning experience.
To reinforce the story, the Body Parts word search exercise is presented by the teacher. The students
are required to find the hidden words in the letter grid as quickly as possible. The teacher divides the
students into small groups beforehand to complete the task. Each group takes turns, promoting active
engagement and teamwork.
Once the students have fully completed the digital story and related vocabulary activities, they are
required to collaboratively create a story of at least two pages. During the story-writing process, one
student may generate visuals using AI powered tools, another may provide voice narration, while the
remaining one or two students focus on composing the story text. This approach promotes teamwork,
creativity, and the effective integration of digital tools in storytelling.

Post-Activity
The teacher carries out the assessment using the Animal ID activity (Annex-3). In this activity, the
students are required to look at the pictures and fill in the gaps, enabling the teacher to evaluate their

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a labelled diagram activity titled Fun with Animals and an Eco-Friendly Suggestions
activity using one of the web tools for matching activities. The teacher also prepares an animation
titled Let’s Protect Nature using one of the animation creation tools (Annex-4).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher guides the students through the Fun with Animals labelled diagram activity. In this activity,
the students are invited to drag and drop pins onto the correct part of animal images to match them
with their names. This interactive task helps the students to improve their vocabulary and visual
recognition skills in a fun and engaging way.
While Activity
After the labelled diagram activity, the students continue with the Let's Protect Nature animation, where
they explore various suggestions for protecting nature (Annex-4). The teacher pauses the animation at
specific points in the story to ask comprehension questions such as, »What do you think about that?”.
This strategy ensures that the students understand the content and encourages active engagement.
Afterwards, the teacher asks the students to work in pairs to brainstorm and write their own
environmental protection suggestions, either by turning to the person next to them or by using a preset
pairing method. Each pair focuses on three of the 3Rs (reuse, reduce, and recycle), coming up with one
specific idea for each category. They can refer to ideas from the story for inspiration, before presenting
their suggestions to the class. This activity helps to reinforce their understanding of the story while
fostering teamwork, critical thinking and creativity.
Post-Activity
After the animation, the teacher moves on to the Eco-Friendly Suggestions activity for the assessment.
In this task, the students are encouraged to read each suggestion carefully and then match it with the
corresponding image using the drag-and-drop method.

Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates an exercise named Find the Animal using one of the web tools for matching
activities. The teacher prepares a Reflection Paper using one of the interactive presentation tools and
brings a hard copy of it to hand out to the students (Annex-5).
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher asks the students to complete the Find the Animal exercise. They look at the shadows of
the animals in the picture, click on a circle to hear an audio clip and then match the description to the
correct animal. This activity helps the students to improve their listening and observation skills, as well
as reinforcing their knowledge of different animals.
While Activity
After the interactive warm-up activity, the teacher introduces a poster creation task. Each pair will
design three digital posters using one of the poster and flyer creation tools, based on the three ideas
they wrote in the previous lesson about the 3Rs (reuse, reduce and recycle). Each poster should include
a clear title, a short explanation, relevant images, and a call to action. Once the posters are complete,
the groups will present them digitally to the class.
Post-Activity
To evaluate the students, the teacher uses the Reflection Paper, which includes questions about the
names of the animal species learnt and suggestions for actions that can be taken to protect nature,
etc., and requires the students to complete it as part of the assessment process (Annex-5).
Homework/Assignment
The teacher sets the students the task of creating a short presentation using an interactive
presentation tool. For this presentation, students must choose two animals and provide information
about them, including their habitat, classification (e.g. mammal, bird or reptile), weight and height. They
will also include photos of the animals, which can be sourced digitally or drawn and coloured in by
hand. Additionally, the students will suggest ways to protect these animals and their habitats. Once
completed, the students are expected to upload their presentations to EBA before the lesson begins,
ensuring they are prepared for class discussions. This activity encourages the students to practise their
research skills, showcase their creativity and use technology effectively, all while engaging with their
classmates.
