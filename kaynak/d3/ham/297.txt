Activities
Before the Lesson
The students are required to complete Vocabulary Exercise 1: Weather and Emotions on EBA. This
activity focuses on identifying and using appropriate vocabulary to describe different weather
conditions. The teacher designs a memory card game named Find the Pairs using a gamification web
tool to reinforce vocabulary through an engaging and interactive learning experience. The teacher
also creates a digital story titled Weather Around the World using one of the digital story creation
tools (Annex-1). Additionally, the teacher designs an Interactive Weather Match exercise using one of
the web tools for matching activities.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher implements the Find the Pairs memory card game as a warm-up activity to activate
the students’ prior knowledge and engage them in the lesson. In this interactive task, the students
are required to memorise the position of all the cards and match each pair correctly. They are
encouraged to complete the task as quickly as possible and try to beat their best score. This activity
enhances vocabulary retention and improves short-term memory and fosters digital literacy skills by
incorporating game-based learning elements.
While Activity
Following the memory card game, the teacher proceeds to the Weather Around the World story, in
which the students learn about the weather in the capitals of different countries (Annex-1). The teacher
then asks the students to remember the weather conditions discussed in the story. For example, they
might be asked, “What is the weather like in Ankara?” to encourage reflection on the information heard.
Next, the teacher divides the class into pairs or small groups and invites each group to choose a city.
Working together, the students then create a weather card depicting the weather conditions in their
chosen city, illustrating it in a similar way to the story. Once their drawings are complete, each group
presents their weather card to the class and explains the weather in their selected city. This activity
reinforces listening comprehension, fosters creative expression and encourages teamwork.

Post-Activity
For the assessment, the teacher incorporates the Interactive Weather Match exercise, in which the
students click on a circle to listen to an audio clip and select the correct weather condition from
the options provided. This activity strengthens listening comprehension skills, helping the students
to accurately associate auditory input with visual representations of weather vocabulary. It also
encourages active engagement by providing an interactive digital learning experience.
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an activity titled Guess the Weather through a gamification web tool to enhance
student engagement and reinforce weather-related vocabulary. Additionally, the teacher designs
a digital story titled Exploring Weather in VR using one of the digital story creation tools (Annex-2).
Finally, the teacher designs an exercise titled Emotions using one of the gamified measurement and
evaluation web tools.
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher implements the Guess the Weather activity as a warm-up task to help the students review
and internalise weather-related vocabulary. In this interactive activity, the students first listen to a
weather-related word or phrase and then drag the letters into the correct positions to unscramble it.
This task supports the development of listening comprehension, spelling and vocabulary recognition,
and enhances digital literacy and engagement through a multisensory learning approach.
While Activity
After the activity, the teacher moves on to the Exploring Weather in VR digital story (Annex-2). In this
story, the students enter a virtual reality classroom and take turns to experience different weather
conditions such as sunny, rainy, snowy, or windy. As they explore the scenes, they describe what they
see and express how the weather makes them feel using simple target sentences (e.g. ‘It’s sunny; I
feel happy on sunny days.’).
Following the story, the teacher organises a Weather & Emotion Station Activity. The classroom is
divided into four stations, each represented by an emoji and labelled with a different emotion: happy
( ), sad ( ), sleepy ( ) and angry ( ). The students then move to the station that best represents
how they felt during their VR weather experience. At each station, they complete a short, guided task.
Station 1 – Happy : Draw or describe a type of weather that makes you happy.
Station 2 – Sad : Match weather icons with emotion words.
Station 3 – Sleepy : Complete the sentence: ‘I feel sleepy when it is...’
Station 4 – Angry : Role-play a short dialogue expressing emotions caused by extreme weather.

Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an exercise named Weather Bowling Challenge using a gamified measurement

3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher uses the Weather Bowling Challenge exercise as a warm-up activity, in which the students
roll a ball towards a set of pins, each labelled with different weather conditions such as sunny, rainy,
or cloudy (Annex-3). If a student answers correctly, the corresponding pin falls, and they earn a point.
If the student is unsure of the answer, the ball rolls past the pin without knocking it down, and the next
student takes the turn. This activity promotes visual recognition and reinforces vocabulary retention in
an interactive and fun way.

While Activity
Following the warm-up activity, the students move on to the Weather/Seasons/Emotions categorisation
task. In this interactive drag-and-drop exercise, the students are asked to drag the words and drop
them into the correct categories. They categorise vocabulary related to weather conditions (e.g. sunny,
rainy), seasons (e.g. winter, summer), and emotions (e.g. happy, sad). This activity supports vocabulary
recall and helps the students to organise concepts visually while practising word recognition and
spelling. The teacher divides the class into groups of four and asks each group to design a digital poster
about the weather, the seasons and emotions. The teams use one of the poster and flyer creation
tools to design their posters. Each team must choose at least three weather conditions, three seasons
and three emotions and write simple sentences in English using these, such as 'It is hot in summer' or
'I feel sleepy on rainy days'. They must also add drawings or images to visually represent each word
or sentence. Once completed, each team leader presents their team's work to the class, explaining
the weather types, seasons and emotions included, and reading the sentences aloud to practise
pronunciation and speaking.
Post-Activity
To assess the students, the teacher uses the Weather and Emotion Quiz, a multiple-choice activity
in which the students are expected to look at the visuals, read the sentence or short text carefully,
and mark the correct option that matches the weather condition or emotion described (Annex-4). This
activity improves the students’ reading comprehension and vocabulary recognition and encourages
them to make connections between visuals and written language.
Homework/Assignment
The students create an interactive digital story about imaginary visits to three cities around the world,
such as London, Tokyo and Paris. In their stories, the students will 'travel' to these cities, observe the
weather and describe how it makes them feel. They are encouraged to enhance their stories by adding
images, icons or animations to visually represent the weather and their emotions in each city. Once
finished, the students submit their work to the teacher via a designated classroom management tool.
