Activities
Before the Lesson
First, the teacher prepares a visual titled Six Corners of Using the Internet using one of the visual
creation and editing tools and prints it out. Then the teacher creates a video titled Pros and Cons
of the Internet using one of the video editing tools (Annex-1). Finally, for the assessment, the teacher
prepares Safety Rules questions using one of the web tools for true-false questions (Annex-2).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher commences the lesson posing the question, “How many hours do you use the internet
per day?” and subsequently invites the students to respond using their fingers. In responses where the
duration of internet usage exceeds two hours, the teacher issues a warning to the students regarding
the potential risks of internet addiction. Following the question, the teacher displays the Six Corners
of Using Internet visual, such as doing research, doing online shopping and reading e-book on the
interactive whiteboard. The teacher then divides the printed version of the visual into six sections,
which are affixed to the six corners of the classroom. Subsequently, the teacher invites the students
to examine the visual on the board and poses the question, “For what purpose do you use the internet
most?”. The students are then invited to move to the corresponding corner for their internet usage,
write their names on the visual and they discuss on the purposes of using internet.
While Activity
Following the discussion, the teacher initiates the Pros and Cons of the Internet video, in which the
students identify the main ideas about the advantages and disadvantages of internet usage (Annex-1).
The teacher uses the video to capture the students’ attention and prepare them for the subsequent
activity. The students are then invited to form two circles, one inside and one outside, in order to apply
the active learning approach of the Fishbowl technique. The inner circle discusses the advantages of
using the internet while the outer circle observes and takes notes. After a set time, the students switch
roles, allowing everyone to participate. The inner circle then discusses the disadvantages of using
the internet while the outer circle observes, fostering critical thinking, active listening and structured
speaking in an engaging way. The teacher provides prompt feedback throughout the activity.

Post-Activity
Following the Fishbowl activity, to assess comprehension, the teacher implements the Safety Rules
questions, wherein the students are invited to meticulously peruse the provided statement and
ascertain its veracity as either true or false, increasing their awareness of safe Internet use (Annex-2).
The teacher conducts a class discussion with the students to establish the reasons behind the veracity
of each statement.
Digital literacy
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
Using one of the online discussion tools, the teacher begins the lesson by using the Round Robin
technique, an active learning approach. The students are required to provide swift responses to
scenarios such as: “You receive a friend request from someone you don’t know. What would you
do?” or “Someone asks you for some personal information. What would you do?” If they are unable to
formulate a response, they can write ‘Pass’. Once all the scenarios have been discussed, the teacher
summarises the key points about internet usage and safety to raise awareness among the students.
While Activity
The teacher then divides the class into six groups, employing the names written on the six corners
of using the Internet visuals. The students who utilise the Internet for the same purpose convene to
prepare a presentation using an interactive presentation tool to explain their purposes and reasons
for using the Internet. The students who use the internet for the same purpose convene to prepare
a presentation using an interactive presentation tool to explain their reasons for using the internet.
They are required to prepare a detailed presentation and attempt to persuade their classmates of
the necessity of their usage. Furthermore, they are expected to list the pros and cons of their internet
usage and, if applicable, create infographics to visualise the data. The teacher emphasises that these
presentations will be delivered to classmates and will offer guidance as required.
Post-Activity
The students deliver their presentations to their classmates and try to persuade them for the necessity
of their internet usage and share the presentation on EBA. The other students then have the opportunity
to express their approval of the presentations in the form of either likes or comments. The presentation
which has the most likes or comments will be declared as the winner.

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a Travel Blog using one of the web page editing tools. The teacher prepares an
activity titled Match the Definitions using one of the web tools for matching activities (Annex-3).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
At the start of the lesson, the teacher poses the following questions: 'Who uses the internet the most in
your family?' and 'What is it used for?' After receiving these responses, the teacher asks the students
to help create a mind map on the interactive whiteboard showing the family members' general daily
internet usage, using one of the mind mapping tools. Once the mind map is complete, the teacher
initiates a discussion about the various purposes of internet usage. The teacher emphasises that
the internet is a valuable source of information and, when used correctly, can be an effective tool
for personal development. However, they also note that it can be harmful to many individuals and,
without proper regulation, can lead to dangerous addiction.
While Activity
The teacher then uses the mind map to show the students the Travel Blog on the interactive whiteboard.
This helps the students to understand what parts a website has. The website has pages such as travel
photos page, travel podcasts page and popular touristic destinations page. The teacher then proceeds
to meticulously examine the pages, informing the students that they would subsequently undertake the
task of constructing the absent components of the website in the following lesson. The teacher focuses
on the differences between the terms “download” and “upload” and provides illustrative examples on
the website.
Post-Activity
To assess comprehension, the teacher implements the activity titled Match the Definitions, in which the
students work in pairs to match internet-related words with their definitions (Annex-3).
Before the Lesson
The teacher makes a Checklist using one of the visual creation and editing tools (Annex-4). The teacher
then prepares a Self-Assessment Form using one of the web tools for self-assessment (Annex-5).
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the Checklist, which lists the various points to consider when designing a website
(Annex-4). The students work in pairs in order to discuss the points on the checklist and consider any
additional points to include.

While Activity
The teacher then follows the checklist to divide the students into five groups. After that, the groups
are asked to build the rest of the website. The teacher provides the students with a template link to
the Travel Blog Template, which serves as a model for their own contributions to the existing content.
The first group is responsible for uploading travel photos, the second group is responsible for “About
Me” page introducing the blogger, the third group is responsible for uploading travel vlogs, the fourth
group is responsible for uploading travel podcasts and the fifth group is responsible for designing
the popular destinations page. The groups may search on the net to contribute to the website, or
they may use their creativity to make alternative contents for the web page. For instance, they may
record their own podcast and upload it to the website, or they may use the podcasts on the net. As
the lesson draws to a close, a collective product will be realised through the collaborative efforts of
the entire class. The students will contribute to the collective endeavour within the larger group while
concurrently engaging in smaller groups.
Post-Activity
Each group presents its contributions on the website, where peers and the teacher provide constructive
feedback.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher posts the Self-Assessment Form on EBA and invites the students to evaluate their own
performance in designing the website (Annex-5).
