Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher shares Tourism video on EBA. The students should watch the video at their own pace. Thus,
they get preliminary information about the theme. They should practice the related vocabulary and
grammar patterns such as kinds of tourist attractions, types of places, “Have you ever been abroad?,
What type of places do you prefer?, Why?”
The teacher creates an animation that two classmates, James and Victor are talking about their
holiday experiences using one of the animation creation tools. Optionally, the teacher can use artificial
intelligence powered tools to write the script of the animation. Additionally, the teacher prepares
questions designed to assess the students’ comprehension of the instructional material (Annex-1).
The teacher creates an age-appropriate infographic Own Your Creation & Respect Others on the topic
of copyright using visual editing and creation tool (Annex-2). The teacher makes this available on EBA
prior to the lesson. This ensures that the students possess preliminary information on this key topic
before the lesson, during which they will conduct online research.
The teacher prepares a set of instructions for the collaborative task, which requires the students to work
in groups of five or six to create a shared digital storybook using a digital storytelling tool (Annex-3).

If I Were a Tourist!
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
1. Ders and Lesson 2 (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the lesson with an icebreaker. The students are asked to write a few questions
on a piece of paper that they can ask their friends sitting next to them. The teacher explains that the
purpose of writing these questions is to find out their friends’ preferences regarding tourist destinations
around the world. The teacher gives examples such as “Would you rather visit the Taj Mahal or Giza
during your summer holiday? Would you rather live in Tokyo or Arizona for a year?”
While Activity
After preparing the students for the topic with a warm-up activity, the teacher informs them that they
will work individually. The students are required to watch and listen to the animation carefully. At the
end of the animation, they are asked to answer some comprehension questions (Annex-1).
Following the question and answer session, the teacher assigns a new task for ongoing lesson. The
students will work in pairs and use the internet to search for their favourite city in the world. Each pair
will then create a digital brochure using the visual creation and editing tools, aiming to convince people
to visit their chosen location. As a reminder, the age-appropriate Own Your Creation & Respect Others
on copyright is already posted on EBA for reference (Annex-2).
The teacher provides guidance to the students on using the visual creation and editing tool, which offers
pre-designed templates to facilitate their work. Furthermore, the following instructions are provided for
creating the digital brochure:
• Use a cover image and a clear message.
• Organise your content with headings.
• Choose easy-to-read fonts.
• Use bullet points, short paragraphs, and strong visuals to break up the text.
• Be careful with copyrights!
Post-Activity
Once all the brochures have been completed, the students are requested to upload their work to EBA
and indicate their preferences by selecting the most attractive work, considering both its visual impact
and the quality of its content.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher uses EBA for assessment and assigns the students Reading Exercise: Tourism. The teacher
explains the students with the objective of a collaborative task to be completed throughout the week.
Subsequently, the teacher provides a comprehensive outline of the task, sharing the list of instructions
that had been prepared in advance of the lesson on EBA (Annex-3). The teacher also informs the students
that they will assess each other at the end of the task via a group assessment form according to their
performance throughout their collaboration.

If I Were a Tourist!
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a How Much Do You Know about the World? spin the wheel game via one of the
gamified measurement and evaluation web tools that includes questions about tourist attractions of
some famous countries around the world (Annex-4).
The teacher creates a digital diary titled My Lovely Diary via digital story creation tool. The topic
concerns 13-year-old Ayşe’s recent holiday. In her diary, Ayşe provides details of her holiday, including
transport, accommodation, and sightseeing. The teacher uses visuals to strengthen the impact of the
instructional material. The teacher prepares comprehension questions which the students are to find
specific information from the diary (Annex-5). It is optional for the teacher to upload the questions to
the web tool for instant feedback and embed the link to the digital story creation tool. This enables the
students to respond to open-ended questions concurrently while reading the diary.
The teacher prepares a Quiz Time! activity using gamified measurement and assessment tool. The
teacher can add multiple-choice and true/false questions as in the examples (Annex-6). The teacher
prepares a group assessment form using the web tool for group assessment to allow the students
assess their peers on their performance for group work (Annex-7).
3. Ders and Lesson 4 (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher leads the students to the IT class for this activity and asks the students to work individually.
The teacher initiates the How Much Do You Know about the World? game prepared via gamified
measurement and evaluation web tools on the interactive whiteboard to motivate the students for the
while activity and to review the pre-lesson task (Annex-4). The teacher directs the students to spin the
wheel for each question. They are informed that a total of 1,000 points will be awarded at the conclusion
of the game, provided that all questions are answered correctly.
While Activity
After the activity that engages the students with the theme of the lesson, the teacher asks the students
to keep working individually. The teacher uses the tool for creating digital boards or post-its. The teacher
adds some sentences on the web tool page such as “I prefer Italy for holiday. I think Italy has the most
beautiful landscapes.”, “I would rather visit Egypt for holiday. In my opinion, Egypt is more adventurous.”,
etc.
The teacher then assigns the students to complete the sentences by adding sticky notes at the bottom
of each blank on the digital board. Once all the students have added their sticky notes, the teacher
initiates a face-to-face discussion as a whole class. The students are asked to explain why they agree
or disagree with their peers’ responses displayed on the board.
5
If I Were a Tourist!
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
After the first activity that encourages the students to express their preferences verbally, the teacher shares
the link for My Lovely Diary on EBA requesting the students to read the diary individually. Subsequently, the
students are required to to answer the comprehension questions (Annex-5). Finally, the teacher displays
the diary and the answers to the corresponding questions on the interactive whiteboard. The teacher
gives feedback on the answers to the questions.
Post-Activity
At the end of the lesson, the teacher assesses student progress using the Quiz Time! activity created with
a gamified measurement and assessment tool (Annex-6). The game starts online, and the teacher waits
for all the students to join in. The teacher explains the game, emphasizing the importance of correct and
quick answers to get more points.
Finally, the teacher checks the collaborative task. The teacher initiates the digital story creation tool on
the interactive whiteboard and run the audiovisual story created by all the students. A group assessment
form allows the students to provide feedback on their peers’ performance (Annex-7).
Homework/Assignment
The teacher also uses EBA for assessment and assigns the students Vocabulary Exercise 3: Tourism and
Listening Exercise: Tourism.
