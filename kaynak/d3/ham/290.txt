Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a video titled Life on the Farm using one of the video editing tools (Annex-1). Then,
for the assessment, the teacher prepares a game titled Reach the Word using one of the gamified
measurement and evaluation web tools.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher begins the lesson by asking the students if they know the difference between city and
village life and then invites them to reflect on this. After this discussion, the teacher plays the Downtown
video about the city and the village on EBA, and the students identify the differences between city
and village life.
While Activity
Following the downtown video, the teacher proceeds to another video titled Life on the Farm, wherein
the students identify simple phrases that are used for the purpose of comparison. (Annex-1). The
teacher uses the video to prepare the students for the subsequent discussion activity and to capture
their attention. Before the discussion begins, the teacher introduces the Reach the Word game, in
which the students match visuals with vocabulary items such as ‘skyscraper’, ‘kiosk’ and ‘crowded’.
This activity serves to review and reinforce the related vocabulary that the students can utilise during
the discussion. The teacher then divides the class into two groups to discuss the following statements:
‘Living in the city improves the quality of life’ and ‘Living in the village improves the quality of life’.
The groups can use chatbots or search engines to conduct detailed research. Within each group,
the students can share roles such as opening speaker, supporting speaker, speaker responding to
arguments from the other side, and closing speaker. Each speaker may also be assigned a specific
time limit. When the groups are ready, they form two circles to begin the discussion. The objective
of this activity is to cultivate critical thinking, active listening and structured speaking while using
comparatives in a captivating manner. The teacher provides ongoing feedback to facilitate student
engagement and progress throughout the activity.

Post-Activity
Following the previous discussion on the use of comparatives, the teacher implements the Marketplace
activity, an approach employed in active learning. Using one of the web tools for instant feedback,
the teacher creates a marketplace and asks the students to write two sentences comparing city and
village life. Once all the students have finished entering their data, the teacher launches the application
in presentation mode via the interactive whiteboard. A volunteer student is then invited to read a
chosen sentence aloud and offer their personal interpretation of it.

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a presentation titled Everyone Is Busy using one of the interactive presentation
tools (Annex-2). Then, for the assessment, the teacher designs a Farm Quiz using one of the gamified
measurement and evaluation web tools (Annex-3).
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the lesson with an Agile, team-based game to foster interaction, spark interest
across diverse learner profiles, and ensure inclusive engagement. The teacher forms groups of four
or five students and provides each group with 20 spaghetti sticks, one yard each of string and tape,
and a marshmallow. Each team then has five minutes to build the tallest possible structure using
the materials provided. The marshmallow must be at the top of the structure. The structure needs
to be free-standing so the students cannot glue it to the table. Additionally, the students cannot rip,
eat or break the marshmallow but they can pierce it. The team that builds the tallest structure which
remains standing for at least 20 seconds wins. The teacher uses an example sentence to compare the
height of the constructions and asks the students to continue comparing them. The objective of this
activity is to promote interaction while practising comparative language.
While Activity
Following this game, the teacher proceeds to the presentation titled Everyone Is Busy, in which the
students identify the expressions and phrases related to present events (Annex-2). The teacher
periodically pauses the presentation to ask questions such as “What is Daisy’s brother doing?”
and “What are the cats doing?”, with the aim of creating an interactive learning environment and
encouraging the students to speak up. To practise with present events further, two students volunteer
to stand at the front of the classroom. One student acts out or mimes a specific event, while the other
asks, “What are they doing?”. The rest of the class is then invited to respond. Throughout the activity,
the teacher provides timely feedback.

Post-Activity
After the previous presentation, to assess the comprehension, the teacher initiates the Farm Quiz, in

Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a matching activity titled What Are They Doing? using one of the web tools for
matching activities (Annex-4). Then, the teacher creates a visual titled Describe What Is Going On using
one of the visual creation and editing tools.
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the What Are They Doing? matching activity, in which the students look at the
visuals and match the given sentences with the corresponding visual (Annex-4). The teacher shares
the link, and the students work in pairs to identify where the characters are and what they are doing.
The objective of this activity is to practise using phrases related to present events.
While Activity
After the matching activity, the teacher selects two volunteer students to act as narrators and displays
the Describe What Is Going On visual exclusively to the narrators. The remaining students are organised
into groups of three; however, they are not permitted to view the visual aids. The narrators must provide
a comprehensive description of the scene and the characters’ actions. The other students then try to
design the scene using one of the visual creation and editing tools available to them. The objective
of this activity is to promote interaction while practising the present continuous tense. Through this
activity, the students learn to collaborate to create a final product.
Post-Activity
Once all groups have finished designing their visuals, they share their work on EBA. The teacher and the
students then compare the visual materials to select the one that most closely resembles the original.

Homework/Assignment
The students are assigned to work in groups of four in order to design two posters. One poster should
depict city life, while the other should depict village life. They are required to use one of the poster and
flyer creation tools for this task. Furthermore, the posters should draw a comparison between city and
village life. The students are assigned to complete Reading Exercise: Carol’s Neighbourhood on EBA.
The students are required to read the passage and answer the questions.
