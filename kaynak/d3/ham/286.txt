Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to complete the following exercises on EBA: Vocabulary Exercise 1: At
the Fair and Reading Exercise: Get Ready to Go to the Funfair! . The teacher designs an activity titled
Guessing Game using one of gamification web tools. The teacher also creates a digital story titled
A Day at the Funfair utilising a digital story creation tool to enhance the students’ engagement and
support language development in the classroom (Annex-1). The teacher prepares an activity named
Listen & Match and an excercise titled Funfair Feelings using web tools for matching activities.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher facilitates the Guessing Game activity, in which the students drag the letters into their
correct positions to unscramble the word or phrase. This activity supports spelling skills, reinforces
vocabulary knowledge, and enhances cognitive abilities such as attention and pattern recognition.
While Activity
The teacher then proceeds with the Listen & Match activity. In this stage, the students are instructed
to listen carefully and drag the sound to the correct picture, promoting both auditory comprehension
and visual recognition skills. Following the matching activity, the teacher transitions to the A Day at the
Funfair digital storytelling activity (Annex-1). During this stage, the teacher displays the digital story. The
students watch and listen as the characters visit different funfair rides and talk about their feelings.
The students focus on key vocabulary and emotions in the story. After watching, the teacher prompts
the students to share which ride they would like to try and how they might feel during the experience.
In addition to these questions, the teacher can foster higher-order thinking skills by asking the students
to reflect on how Emily feels at the beginning and end of the story, suggest a new ride to include, or
describe a funfair in their own city. These questions encourage the students to analyse characters’
emotions, be creative, relate the story to their own experiences, and evaluate key moments in the
narrative.
Post-Activity
For the evaluation stage, the teacher continues with the Funfair Feelings activity, in which the students
listen to the audio on the screen and drag and drop the word they hear under the positive feelings or
negative feelings category.

Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an activity titled Find What is Behind using one of the gamified measurement
and evaluation web tools. The teacher also designs an animation titled Fun at the Fair using one of
animation creation tools (Annex-2). Additionally, the teacher prepares a Find the Pair activity using a
gamification web tool.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the Find What is Behind activity. In this game, an image slowly begins to appear
on the screen. The instruction is simple: Buzz in when you can answer the question. This encourages
the students to pay close attention as the image gradually becomes clearer. The aim is to recognise
the picture before anyone else. Once a student thinks they know what it is, they should buzz in and
give their answer. This fun and competitive activity promotes visual focus, quick thinking, and active
engagement in the classroom.
While Activity
Following the gamification activity, the students watch the animation titled Fun at the Fair, in which
different characters present their posters about their favourite funfair activities (Annex-2). In the
animation, each student shows their poster, talks about their favourite ride, and describes how they
feel. This helps the students see examples of English presentations and learn useful expressions for
discussing personal preferences and feelings. After watching the animation, the teacher prompts
the students with questions such as, “Which poster did you like the most?” and “Which funfair activity
would you like to try?” to support information recall, encourage personal expression, and foster spoken
English practice.
Additionally, the teacher can ask simple reflective questions to promote speaking and creative
thinking, such as: “Do you like the same ride or a different one?”, “Which ride is the most fun for you?”,
“Which ride is fun but slow?”, “Can you make a new funfair ride?”, and “What would you name it?”.
After completing the animation activity, the teacher guides the students to create their own posters
reflecting their favourite funfair activities by using one of the poster and flyer creation tools. The
students are encouraged to use relevant vocabulary and visual elements in their designs. Upon
finishing their posters, each student presents their work to a classmate, describing the content and
explaining their choices. This activity allows the students to practise both their creative and speaking
skills in an interactive and supportive environment. The teacher observes and provides feedback to
help the students effectively communicate their ideas in English.
To support peer feedback, the students can also upload their posters to a shared digital board. The
teacher may introduce simple feedback guidelines, such as using polite language and avoiding
personal remarks. Incorporating varied feedback formats such as text, audio, or video can enhance
student engagement and support diverse expression.

Post-Activity
For the assessment, the teacher moves on to the Find the Pair activity. In this activity, the students
listen to sentences about funfair experiences such as liking the Ferris wheel, eating popcorn or cotton
candy, and expressing preferences for different rides. They tap a pair of tiles at a time to reveal if
they are a match. This activity helps improve the students’ listening comprehension, memory, and
matching skills, as they practice listening for specific information and connecting related ideas.
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares an activity called Funfair Favourites using a web tool for matching activities, and
another activity titled Funfair Days using one of the web tools for fill-in-the-blank questions (Annex-3).
The teacher also designs a third activity, Let’s Compare , using one of the interactive presentation
tools.
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces the Funfair Favourites activity to activate the students at the start of the lesson.
The students are asked to click the icon on the picture and listen to the audio, then choose the correct
sentence about the image. After the activity, the teacher initiates a class discussion and asks the
students questions about the different funfair activities. The students are encouraged to share their
thoughts and opinions, and the teacher listens to their answers, provides feedback, and helps them
express their preferences in English. Next, the teacher encourages the students to compare the funfair
activities by asking questions such as: “Which ride is more exciting—the bumper cars or the ghost
train?” and “Which funfair snack is sweeter—cotton candy or popcorn?”
While Activity
Following the initial stage, the teacher presents the Let’s Compare activity. The students first look at
the slide to review the comparative form and see example sentences. The teacher explains how to use
comparatives and models a few examples. Then, the teacher asks the students to stand up and form
two circles using the onion ring technique, so that each student faces a partner.
The teacher gives each pair two funfair words, such as “roller coaster” and “carousel.” Each student
takes turns comparing the two words using comparative adjectives, for example, “The roller coaster
is more exciting than the carousel.” After each round, the students rotate to face a new partner and
discuss a new pair of words provided by the teacher. This process continues until all students have
practised making comparative sentences with different partners and words. The teacher monitors
the activity, provides feedback, and encourages the students to use the target language actively.

Post-Activity
For the assessment, the teacher continues with the Funfair Days activity. The students are asked to
drag and drop the pins to their correct place on the image (Annex-3). This activity helps the students
review vocabulary related to funfair items and improves their ability to identify and locate objects in
English. It also makes learning more interactive and engaging.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher asks the students to design a single digital poster that includes their three favourite
activities at the fair. The students are encouraged to use one of the poster and flyer creation tools to
add images, keywords, and creative elements. After completing their posters, the students record
a short video to share their work and discuss their reasons for choosing the activities. Finally, the
students share both their digital posters and video recordings on EBA.
