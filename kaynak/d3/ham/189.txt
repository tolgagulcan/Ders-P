Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares a Me in the Future presentation using one of the interactive presentation tools
(Annex-1).
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates a collaborative mind mapping activity by sharing a mind mapping tool with
the class. Centred around the theme of “DREAMS,” the students are invited to contribute their future
aspirations to a shared digital canvas. This interactive exercise fosters collective visioning and creates
a dynamic visual representation of the class’s hopes and ambitions.
While Activity
The activity commences with a collective reflection on the shared dream mind map, stimulating
discussion and goal-oriented thinking. The teacher introduces the core topic, future plans, through
targeted questioning. A comprehensive Me in the Future presentation follows, illuminating diverse
career paths and life options with illustrative dialogues. To solidify understanding and encourage
active participation, the students engage in pair or small group discussions, responding to teacher
prepared questions related to the presented content (Annex-1).
Post-Activity
The students collaborate in pairs to construct imaginative dialogues set in the future. They will work
together to create characters, settings, and plausible future scenarios. The dialogues should incorporate
a variety of future tense structures and vocabulary. Once completed, pairs can practice their dialogues
and get ready to present them to the class in the next lesson.
Before the Lesson
The students are asked to complete Vocabulary Exercise 1: Dreams and Vocabulary Exercise 2: Dreams
to enhance learning before the lesson. The teacher prepares a peer assessment form and informs the
students that they will evaluate each other’s presentations (Annex-2).

2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The students collaborate in pairs, imagining themselves as journalists in the future. The task involves
creating captivating headlines that forecast potential developments or breakthroughs. Whether they
focus on groundbreaking scientific discoveries, or astonishing technological advancements, the students
are challenged to use vivid language and future tense verbs to create engaging and imaginative news
headlines.
While Activity
The lesson commences with a review of a sample future predictions presentation, setting the stage for
collaborative learning. The students are then paired to revisit their previously created dialogues, which
serve as the foundation for their presentations. Utilising one of the interactive presentation tools, pairs
construct visual and textual presentations. Following the presentations, the students engage in a peer
evaluation process, providing constructive feedback on content, organization, and language use.
Post-Activity
Following the project presentations, the students undertake a self-devised quiz to assess their learning.
Subsequently, the group engages in a discussion analysing the various concepts and linguistic choices
employed in the presentations. Peer feedback on individual work is also provided (Annex-2).
Homework/Assignment
To enhance the students’ reading and listening comprehension, the teacher assigns Reading Exercise:
Dreams and Listening Exercise: Dreams on EBA.
Before the Lesson
The teacher wants the students to view Dreams on EBA before the lesson. The teacher prepares questions
using one of the web tools for instant feedback.
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher brings a crystal ball into the classroom and puts this crystal ball on the table. The teacher
assumes the role of a fortune teller, gazing into a crystal ball to predict the future. To enhance student
engagement, the teacher invites several students to participate, crafting imaginative predictions about
their future based on the crystal ball’s supposed insights. These sentences can be structured in this way:
“You will run your own business.”, “You will probably receive a scholarship to a good university.”, “You will
move abroad.”, etc.

While Activity
The teacher wants the students to create a digital story by one of the digital story creation tools. The
students will work in groups to complete this activity. The teacher introduces the digital story creation
tool on the interactive whiteboard. The students are instructed to write down story scripts in order to
express their predictions about the world in the year 2100. They should use their imaginations and think
about possible advancements in technology, science, environment, and society.
Post-Activity
Once they have finished writing their predictions, the teacher initiates a class discussion on the students’
future aspirations. To encourage dynamic interaction, the students are tasked with articulating their
goals utilising web tools for instant feedback. This exercise promotes immediate feedback and peer
engagement as the students share their visions. The teacher serves as a facilitator, fostering thoughtful
dialogue and reflection on the students’ plans and projections. They can collect all their stories about
the future and put them in a time capsule then bury it in the school yard to uncover in 2050 altogether.
Before the Lesson
The teacher tells the students that at the end of the lesson, they will be assessed on their participation
(Annex-3).
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The students are divided into teams and presented with a list of future-related terms. One student from
each team then acts out a chosen term, while the rest of the team attempts to decipher the meaning.
The first team to correctly guess the word or phrase earns a point. This dynamic game format not only
reinforces future tense vocabulary but also enhances the students’ listening and observation skills,
creating a lively and interactive learning experience.
While Activity
The teacher begins with a class discussion about dreams and aspirations. The teacher poses
questions as following: “What are your dreams for the future?”, “Why are dreams important?”, etc. Then
the teacher writes the students’ answers on the board. The teacher briefly reviews the future tense
rules using “will” and provides examples on the board: “I will become a doctor.”, “I will travel abroad.”,
“I will meet a famous person.”, etc. The teacher shares a few sentences and a short paragraph about
own dreams using the future tense: “In the future, I will write a book. I will travel to many countries and
learn about different cultures. I will help people with my stories and inspire them to follow their dreams.”
Then the teacher wants the students to write a paragraph about their own dreams. The teacher gives
writing prompts the students to start their paragraphs. For instance, “In the future, I will...”, “I will achieve
my dreams by...”, etc. The students write their paragraphs individually, using the future tense.

Post-Activity
After writing their paragraphs about dreams, the teacher introduces one of the tools for creating
digital boards or post-its to the class, explaining its functions. The teacher creates a link where the
students can post their paragraphs. The students then type their paragraphs into the tool, allowing
them to read each other’s dreams.
Following this independent reading phase, a few students are selected to read their paragraphs
aloud to the class. This sparks a discussion about shared dreams, with a focus on providing positive
feedback. To further encourage interaction, the students are paired up to exchange their paragraphs
and offer feedback to each other. The teacher emphasises the importance of checking for the correct
use of the future tense during this peer review process.
At the end of the lesson, the teacher implements a student assessment form to evaluate the students
(Annex-3). According to the students’ answers, the teacher provides feedback.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher instructs the students to compose a letter addressed to their future selves, articulating
their aspirations and outlining their plans for achieving them, employing future tense verb forms.
