Activities
Before the Lesson
First, the teacher designs a Why Is It Important? poster using one of the poster and flyer creation tools
and prints out the poster (Annex-1). Then, the teacher creates An Interesting Day at the Library story
using one of the digital story creation tools (Annex-2). Next, for the assessment, the teacher creates
What Happened? multiple-choice questions using one of the web tools for multiple-choice questions
(Annex-3). Lastly, the teacher assigns the students to conduct research about the importance of reading
books for our self-development.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the Why Is It Important? poster on the interactive whiteboard and invites the
students to examine it closely and meanwhile, the teacher hangs the poster on the class board (Annex-1).
The teacher poses questions such as “Do you enjoy reading books?” and “Why is reading important?”
to ascertain the students’ reading habits. The teacher then invites the students to form two circles on
the board, one inside and the others outside. The students are required to engage in discourse on the
significance of reading for our self-development to subsequently convey their research findings to their
peers.
While Activity
After the discussion, the teacher initiates the story titled An Interesting Day at the Library, where the
students have to understand short, simple sentences and expressions about past events with definite
time (Annex-2). At regular intervals throughout the reading of the story, the teacher interrupts and
emphasises the use of past tense forms of verbs. The students are asked to note the new verbs in their
notebooks, while the teacher provides a detailed explanation of the rules for converting verbs into past
tense. Additionally, the teacher poses questions such as “Do you go to the library?” and “What kind of
books do you read?” with the objective of fostering an interactive learning environment and encouraging
the students to express themselves. The teacher provides prompt feedback throughout the course of the
activity.
Post-Activity
Following the story, to assess comprehension, the teacher implements the What Happened? multiple-
choice questions, where the students are required to answer the multiple choice questions related to the
story (Annex-3).

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates Chat Cards visuals using one of the visual creation and editing tools. The teacher
prints the visual content and cuts it into the form of cards (Annex-4).
2. Ders ( IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the Chat Cards visuals and requests that the students examine the questions
briefly (Annex-4). The teacher places the printed chat cards on the table and invites the students to
work in pairs. One student takes a chat card from the table, poses a question, and the other student
responds. The teacher observes the students’ communication skills and provides immediate feedback.
While Activity
Following the chat cards activity, the teacher initiates the An Interesting Day at the Library story again,
reminds the important parts, and shares the story on EBA. The students work in groups of four using one
of the tools for creating digital boards or post-its and are given the task of continuing the story, using the
simple past tense to formulate a satisfactory conclusion. The students are encouraged to demonstrate
creativity in their writing and drawing the visuals by crafting an ending that is both remarkable and
enjoyable. Furthermore, the students may vocalise their stories. The teacher walks around the students
and provides prompt feedback throughout the course of the activity. Upon completion of the writing
task, the students are required to share their stories on EBA wall. Each student needs to engage with the
submitted stories, offering constructive feedback in the form of comments. The class then votes for the
story they think is the best, and the teacher prints out the story that is selected and displays it on the
class board.
Post-Activity
The teacher initiates Grammar Exercise 1: Bookworms on EBA, where the students match the sentences
with pictures. The teacher then emphasises the importance of reading in our lives and invites the
students to initiate a book collecting campaign throughout the school. The ensuing details will be
elucidated: Firstly, the exhibition corridor, in which all the steps of the campaign will be displayed; and
secondly, the objective of the campaign, which is the popularisation of reading. The students are then
assigned the task of designing posters for the following lesson emphasising the importance of reading.
The dissemination of these posters is intended for exhibition corridors to promote the campaign, and
the students make announcements to promote the collection of books for the school. A designated
reading area will be established within the exhibition corridor for the use of all students. The teacher
will act as a guide during the implementation of the campaign.

Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a Bookworms word cloud using one of the word cloud creation tools. Then, the
teacher creates a Find the Visuals activity using one of the visual creation and editing tools. Next, the
teacher designs a My Room visual using one of the visual creation and editing tools (Annex-5). Lastly, for
the assessment, the teacher creates a Where Is It? visual using one of the visual creation and editing
tools (Annex-6).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher presents the Bookworms word cloud and invites the students to analyse it, focusing on
words such as “novel”, “author” and “library”. Subsequent to the word cloud, the teacher commences
the Find the Visuals activity, wherein the students recall the words from the word cloud and identify the
corresponding visuals. The student who identifies the correct visuals with the greatest expediency will
be declared the winner. The teacher may give a book to the winner as a gift.
While Activity
Subsequent to the presentation of the visual materials, the teacher displays the My Room visual, wherein
the students are tasked with identifying the locations of various items (Annex-5). The teacher then
proceeds to emphasise the prepositions of place in the visual, followed by the presentation of example
sentences that illustrate the application of these prepositions in the classroom setting. Subsequently, the
teacher displays the Where Is It? visual, which prompts the students to examine the visual and discuss
the locations of objects (Annex-6). Finally, the teacher invites the students to work in pairs, discuss the
locations of the objects, and fill in the blanks with the appropriate prepositions.
Post-Activity
Following the activity, the teacher invites the students to participate in a game known as “Hide an
Object”, in which the students are required to describe the location of an object utilising prepositions. The
teacher requests a volunteer student to leave the classroom. While the volunteer is away, the teacher
encourages the remaining students to select an object to hide. Upon the volunteer’s return, the rest of
the students formulate sentences employing prepositions of place to assist the volunteer in finding the
object. The volunteer utilises these clues to find the object.
Homework/Assignment
The students are assigned to organise the exhibition corridor, popularise the book collecting campaign
and design a small reading corner in the exhibition corridor.
