Activities
Before the Lesson
Prior to the lesson, the teacher prepares a presentation titled Appearance and Personality to introduce
the vocabulary using one of the interactive presentation tools (Annex-1). The teacher creates a Puzzle
activity. The teacher designs an activity named What’s on the Note? (Annex-2). The teacher writes a note
with one letter in each square , including some punctuation and letters on the puzzle to provide clues.
Since the students will solve it in groups, the teacher makes a few more copies and leaves the papers in
different parts of the classroom before the students arrive.

Little Detectives: What’s on the Note?
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The students come to the classroom and find the papers for the What’s on the Note? activity left by the
teacher (Annex-2). They ask the teacher what they are, and the teacher explains they’ll find a password,
to be revealed at the activity’s end. To stimulate strategic thinking and foster collaboration, the students
embark on a timed Puzzle challenge. Divided into groups, the students tackle various letter, detective,
or password puzzles within a time limit. Upon completion, they’re invited to tape their puzzles together
and check the back, revealing the unit title and scenario name, which they then write on the board. This
engaging introduction sparks curiosity and provides initial information about the lesson’s topic and
scenario content, prompting the teacher to display relevant visuals around the classroom.
While Activity
The teacher initiates a shape and drawing tool on the interactive whiteboard and draws a winding
path, providing a creative way to practise vocabulary. After marking points along the path, the
teacher introduces the topic of appearance and personality, explaining the meaning of each word
and providing examples. The students are then tasked with finding and listing additional related
words. Working in groups, they compete to find the most words, which they then write on the path and
pronounce together. Following this, the teacher introduces the concept of antonyms, using coloured
ropes to visually connect antonyms. The students then match words with their antonyms, and group
representatives complete the activity on the board. This approach effectively engages the students,
familiarises them with vocabulary, and reinforces learning through repetition and active participation.
Post-Activity
After this activity, the teacher delivers the Appearance and Personality presentation (Annex-1). The
students recognise the vocabulary through the accompanying visuals and study the sample sentences.
The teacher provides a brief explanation, discussing the use of “have got/has got” and how to form
sentences describing features of appearance. Then the teacher introduces the question pattern, “What
does she/he look like?” The remainder of the presentation will be covered in lesson 3.

Little Detectives: What’s on the Note?
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
Before the Lesson
In preparation for the lesson, the teacher writes descriptions of people with different appearances and
places them in surprise egg boxes (Annex-3). To make the activity more appealing, the teacher can
also put small stickers or sweets in the boxes. If there are no boxes available, the teacher can write the
sentences on coloured wooden sticks or simply on pieces of paper. The teacher places extra pieces of
paper in some of the boxes. These papers contain notes such as “the 3rd letter is A for the 10th space”.
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher hides surprise egg boxes or wooden sticks, each containing a brief description of a person
with a distinctive appearance, in various locations around the classroom (Annex-3). The teacher then
displays one of the boxes to the students, informing them that the boxes or sticks are hidden somewhere
in the classroom and inviting them to find them. The students then undertake a search for the materials
in the classroom. The student who finds the boxes or sticks is responsible for delivering them to the
teacher. This approach engages the students’ anticipation and curiosity prior to the lesson, ensuring
they are optimally prepared for the upcoming learning activity.
While Activity
The teacher introduces an engaging activity to the students and demonstrates how to do it. Selecting
a surprise egg, the teacher opens it and reads the sentence inside aloud. Using the description
provided, the teacher begins to draw a person’s image with a shape and drawing tool, saving the
completed drawing on the interactive whiteboard. The teacher then verifies the drawing by repeating
the description from the paper. Following this demonstration, the students are invited to participate in
the same activity. Each student takes a turn coming to the board, selecting an egg box, and opening
it to reveal the description inside. They then draw the features described on the board. This process
continues until all the surprise egg boxes have been opened and all descriptions have been read aloud.
As the students open the boxes, they announce the letters, which the teacher writes on the board for the
What’s on the Note? activity (Annex-2). This collaborative and interactive approach not only reinforces
the lesson but also fosters a sense of curiosity and engagement amongst the students. If there are no
surprise egg boxes and no preparation is possible, the students can describe a visual feature to create
an image.
Post-Activity
The teacher guides the students through the Listening Exercise: New Friends on EBA. They then complete
the task together.

Little Detectives: What’s on the Note?
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
Before the Lesson
The teacher ensures that the Appearance and Personality presentation is ready for the lesson (Annex-1).
Additionally, the teacher designs a self-evaluation form and a group performance evaluation form
(Annex-4 and Annex-5). The teacher informs the students that they, along with their peers, will evaluate
their performance at the end of the lesson.
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher and the students form a circle. They can carry out this activity in the school garden, before
returning to the classroom. Alternatively, if there are many students, the teacher can split the class in
two and play the game this way. The teacher stands in the middle as the students play a game where
each person names a physical characteristic. Those with the same characteristics change places.
Those who do not fit in stay in the middle. For example, if the teacher says, “I have dark brown hair.” and
everyone with dark brown hair switches places. Anyone unable to find a place remains in the centre.
Then a student might say, “I have hazel eyes.” and those with hazel eyes switch places. The person who
is unable to find a place remains in the central position.
While Activity
The teacher informs the students that they will evaluate each other’s performance at the end of the
lesson emphasising the importance of being active. The game from the end of the Appearance and
Personality presentation used in previous lessons is then initiated (Annex-1).
The teacher reads the description aloud and the students identify the first person. Afterwards, the
teacher asks to find the person they have just read about. Once they have found the person, the students
ask for another clue on the slide. They will use the letters in the names of the people they find to form a
password, which increases their curiosity further. This activity can be done collectively or turned into a
game. It combines both digital tools and instructional materials.
To play the game, the teacher divides the class into two groups. A desk with a game bell is placed at the
front of the classroom. The teacher asks the students to read the description on the slide, after which
the first student to correctly guess the person rings the bell. Points are awarded to the group of the
student’s group if they guess correctly. To emphasise the importance of correct answers and minimise
random guesses, the teacher awards double points for accurate answers. For example, if a student
says, “This is John.” and it is the correct answer, their group earns ten points. The teacher then asks,
“Can you explain?” If the student responds with an explanation such as, “It is written that the person has
blond hair and blue eyes; the person being described is John.” this explanation is correct, they earn an
additional ten points. The students play the game according to these rules and determine the winning
group congratulating them with applause. As they find people, they take notes on the clues given and
gradually complete the What’s on the Note? activity (Annex-2). After the game, the teacher concludes
the slide show.

Post-Activity
The teacher asks, “What’s on the note?” and the students find the note by arranging the letters. The
group that finds the note can be rewarded. In this scenario, the note reads: “The key to success is: Never
give up!” The teacher can create different notes or passwords by changing the slides or papers.
The teacher then distributes the self-evaluation form and the group performance evaluation form
(Annex-4 and Annex-5). The students evaluate themselves and each of their group members. They then
review the results together, provide feedback to each other, and identify their strengths and areas for
improvement in group work and activities.
Problem solving
Before the Lesson
The teacher uses one of the tools for creating digital boards or post-its to create sections equal to the
number of the students. During the lesson, each student will be expected to add a celebrity to their
section and introduce them. The teacher prepares an Appearance Quiz for the last lesson with one of
the gamified measurement and evaluation web tools (Annex-6).
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays a photo of a celebrity to capture the students’ attention. The teacher asks questions
about who the celebrity is and what they look like. After discussing this with the students for a while, the
teacher announces that each student will introduce a celebrity they like.
While Activity
The teacher begins by launching the web tool for creating digital boards or post-its displaying the
sections assigned to each student. The students are then required to select their favourite singer, actor,
or celebrity and upload a photo of that person. They can also write a text comparing two famous people.
Next, the teacher guides the students in writing an introduction to their chosen celebrity, incorporating
all the information they have learned in previous lessons.
As the students work on their tasks, the teacher moves around the classroom, offering assistance to
those who need it and observing their progress. Sufficient time is provided for the students to complete
their assignments. Once the time is up, the teacher displays the link on the interactive whiteboard and
organises a peer feedback session. Volunteer students read their work aloud, and classmates provide
constructive feedback, highlighting strengths and offering suggestions for improvement. This approach
encourages active listening and fosters a supportive learning environment.

Post-Activity
The teacher initiates the Appearance Quiz (Annex-6). The teacher ensures that the quiz covers previous
topics and includes various question types. The students complete the activity in the IT classroom and
share the link with their parents. The online quiz enables the teacher to assess what the students have
learnt, identify incorrect answers, and determine who needs additional support. Based on the results,
the teacher can decide which topics to review in the next lesson.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher assigns an engaging homework task that uses artificial intelligence to generate visuals. After
demonstrating how to use the artificial intelligence powered tool, the teacher encourages the students
to design a person using the information they have learnt. The students save their AI-generated images
and share them on EBA wall, accompanied by their written descriptions from the web tool. The teacher
then reviews the images and written work submitted, preparing to discuss them in the next lesson.
