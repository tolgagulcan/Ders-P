## 5. Sinif/Ingilizce/Life in the Neighbourhood & City - Neighbourhood at a Glance.pdf

KAV:Places for recreation and attraction, Neighbourhood, Attractions
Learning Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher chooses a song, “Welcome to My Town”, related to the theme and the sub-theme of the unit.
Then, the teacher prepares a digital story titled Exploring the City by using an interactive presentation tool
(Annex-1). Also, the teacher prepares a drag and drop matching activity titled Places Vocabulary Matching
by using web tools for matching activities (Annex-2).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
To engage the students and activate their listening and visual memory skills related to the unit vocabulary,
the teacher plays the song “Welcome to My Town” on the interactive whiteboard, so that the students can
both see and hear the visuals and lyrics of the song. The teacher asks the students to identify and write
down any words from the unit they hear in the song (e.g. places in the town, buildings, or landmarks). After
listening, the students sing the song together with the teacher to improve their pronunciation in an enjoyable
way. Then, the teacher asks the students guiding questions like, “Which place in the song would you like to
visit, and why?” This encourages speaking and connects it with personal experience.
While Activity
To reinforce unit vocabulary, build contextual understanding, and improve listening skills, the teacher
displays the digital story, Exploring the City, containing sentences built around the unit vocabulary (Annex-
1). The students can hear correct pronunciation and connect meaning to visuals. Then, the teacher asks
comprehension questions like ‘’ Where is the local shop? What can you do near the coast? And then, the
teacher asks critical thinking questions like, “Which place in the town do you think is the most useful for the
community? Why?” “What are the similarities and differences between this place and a place in your city?”
Post-Activity
The teacher reinforces the vocabulary that the students have learnt during the lesson by using the Places
Vocabulary Matching Activity (Annex-2). The teacher displays the activity on the interactive whiteboard.
This activity helps the students review and consolidate vocabulary about the places in the town in a fun
and interactive way.
Comparison, Interpretation, Listening/Watching-comprehension, Speaking-

Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares guiding questions, so the students connect the lesson theme to their own experiences
through these questions. Then, the teacher prepares a digital story titled My New Neighbourhood using an
interactive presentation tool to present the topic (Annex-3). Afterwards, the teacher designs a follow-up
activity named Discover Lisa’s Place about the story, using web tools for multiple-choice questions to check
the students’ comprehension (Annex -4).
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher gives a short introduction to the students about comparatives. After the introduction, the teacher
organises a short and fun warm-up activity called “Yes or No Jump”. The teacher reads simple comparative
sentences related to neighbourhood places. If the sentence is correct, the students jump and say “Yes”.
If the sentence is incorrect, they stay still and say “No”. Then, the teacher says the correct sentence and
the students repeat it together. This active activity helps the students revise comparative structures in an
enjoyable way and supports their well-being by encouraging movement and participation.
While Activity
The teacher asks the students guiding questions such as “Do you live in a new house or in the same house?”,
“Is your neighbourhood bigger or smaller than before?”, and “Is your home closer to your school or further
away?”. Then, the teacher engages the students in the digital story, My New Neighbourhood (Annex-3).
Before watching the digital story, the teacher asks prediction questions, such as “What do you think the story
will be about?” or “What kind of place do you think Lisa has moved to?”. After viewing the story, the teacher
uses a Think–Pair–Share activity to support student interaction, collaboration, and communication. First,
the students think individually about Lisa’s new neighbourhood and how they feel about her daily life there.
Then, the students work in pairs and share their ideas with each other. Finally, one student from each pair
shares their common opinion with the class. During the activity, the students use simple sentence chunks
such as “I think Lisa’s neighbourhood is …” and “In my opinion, it is better / worse than …” to express their ideas.
Post-Activity
The teacher presents the online quiz on the interactive whiteboard. The students complete the Discover
Lisa’s Place activity to check their comprehension and practise key vocabulary (Annex-4).

Before the Lesson
The teacher first prepares an activity named Rooms & Furniture Flashcards with vocabulary about rooms
and furniture by using visual creation and editing tools. Then, the teacher creates a house sketch on the
interactive whiteboard tools titled My Dream House-Architecture Activity for the students to use during the
activity. The teacher prepares a Self-Assessment Stop and Check form using the visual creation and editing
tool to help the students evaluate what they have learnt at the end of the lesson (Annex-5). For the homework
the teacher also prepares a guideline named Your Digital Welcome Kit by using one of the poster and flyer
creation tools to show the students what to do and what to include in their Digital Welcome Kit (Annex-6).
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays Rooms & Furniture Flashcards with vocabulary about rooms and furniture, such as
kitchen, bedroom, sofa, bed, and fridge. The students make simple sentences using each word. This helps
the students reinforce the new vocabulary before starting the main activity.
While Activity
The teacher first demonstrates the students how to use the interactive whiteboard tool, explaining how to
draw shapes, add text, and move objects. The teacher introduces My Dream House-Architecture Activity
which the students complete as a group work. Then, students use a house sketch and imagine they are
architects designing a home. They design the rooms carefully and place furniture and objects in each room
according to the vocabulary they have learnt. The students write the names of the rooms and furniture
clearly on the sketch. This activity allows the students to practice the vocabulary, understand the function
of each room, and be creative in planning their own house design.
Post-Activity
The students present their houses to the class. They describe the rooms, furniture, and objects using the new
vocabulary. The rest of the class listens and engages by asking simple questions about the houses. Then,
the students fill in the Self-Assessment Stop and Check form (Annex-5).
Homework/ Assignment
The teacher asks the students to prepare a Your Digital Welcome Kit including a welcome message,
neighbourhood information, and helpful daily life tips for a new neighbour who has just moved to the town
(Annex-6). The teacher shares a guideline with the students to show them what to do and what to include
in their Digital Welcome Kit.
Interpretation, Decision-making, Speaking-expression, Writing-expression,
Vocabulary: Selection and use, Creativity, Questioning, Self-awareness,
