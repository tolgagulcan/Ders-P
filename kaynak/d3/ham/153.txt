KAV:Future, Plans, Travel, Exploration, Digital learning, Communication
Learning Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares several digital resources to increase student motivation and support active participation.
First, the teacher initiates the lesson by launching an online map application on the interactive board and
selects fascinating locations, such as the Grand Canyon, Cappadocia, and the Alps Mountains, to explore
during the lesson. The teacher also prepares a presentation titled Where to Go Next? using an interactive
presentation tool with visuals related to the vocabulary of the lesson.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
At the beginning of the lesson, the teacher displays the online map application on the interactive board.
Together, the class explores our planet by zooming into fascinating places such as the Grand Canyon,
Cappadocia, and the Alps Mountains. The teacher asks guiding questions: “What can you see here?”, “Which
place would you like to visit?”, and “Why do you think this place is important to protect?”.
While Activity
The teacher presents the Where to Go Next? presentation to the students, showing visuals of different
destinations and related vocabulary. The students observe the images and identify the target words. The
teacher asks the students to give short examples using the words in a sentence. Through this activity, the
students practise using target vocabulary and grammar in meaningful sentences.
Post-Activity
The students work in pairs to practise speaking by asking and answering questions about the places in the
presentation, such as: “Which place have you visited?”, “Which place would you like to see?”, and “Which place
did you like the most?”. This helps the students use the vocabulary in meaningful conversations, connect it
to their real-life experiences, and improve their speaking and communication skills.

Before the Lesson
The teacher designs several digital flashcards titled Word Voyage, using a visual creation and editing tool,
with key vocabulary items such as island, desert, coral reef, beach, national park, and holiday. In the next
step, the teacher prepares four stations using the station rotation technique, ensuring each station has a
specific activity. Future Plans Station is created using one of the web tools for fill-in-the-blank questions
(Annex-1). Matching Activities Station and Values Reflection Station are created using web tools for matching
activities (Annex-2). Quick Quiz Station is created using one of the gamified measurement and evaluation
web tools (Annex-3). The teacher also plans timing for each station (5 minutes) and prepares instructions
for smooth rotation.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the Word Voyage digital flashcards with key vocabulary from the unit, such as beach,
desert, coral reef, island, mountain, national park, and holiday. The students review these flashcards individually
or in pairs at the beginning of the lesson. After reviewing the digital flashcards, the teacher models the
pronunciation of the key vocabulary aloud. The students first repeat the words chorally as a whole class,
then practise saying them in small groups and finally pronounce them individually. This repetition takes
place together with the visual flashcards displayed on the interactive whiteboard, supporting accurate
pronunciation, listening skills, and vocabulary retention.
While Activity
At the beginning of the lesson, the students recall what they learnt from the previous lesson and use that
knowledge to complete new digital tasks in rotation station technique. The students are divided into small
cooperative groups, and the activity is organised as a competition. Each station lasts 5 minutes, and each
station has a station leader who is responsible for checking answers, recording points, and ensuring fair
play. At the Future Plans Station, the students complete a fill-in-the-blank activity with the correct form of
“am/is/are going to” to describe their future holiday plans, while the station leader records correct answers
for scoring (Annex-1). At the Matching Activities Station, the students match each picture with the correct
word with the station leader verifying answers and awarding points. At the Values Reflection Station, the
students read each sentence and group it under the correct value: Friendship, Responsibility, Patience or
Cleanliness, while the student monitor checks the groupings and records points (Annex-2). At the Quick Quiz
Station, they complete a short interactive quiz to review grammar and vocabulary, with the station leader
checking answers and noting points earned (Annex -3). At the end of all rotations, the points from each
station are summed, and the group with the highest total score is announced as the winner.

Post-Activity
After completing all stations, each group briefly shares what they did and learnt, while the teacher asks
short questions to reinforce learning, such as: “Which activity did you enjoy the most?”, “Which new word will
you remember and use next?”, or “Can you explain one task your group found interesting?”. This encourages
reflection on station activities and allows the students to verbalise their learning.
Framework Analysis, Critical thinking, Speaking-expression
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a gamified, puzzle-like vocabulary game, Destination Decoder, using one of the
gamification web tools. The teacher creates a Reflective Journal Guideline for writing reflective journals,
using a visual creation and editing tool, including step-by-step instructions (Annex-4). A digital board,
Future Travel Projects, is created using one of the tools for creating digital boards or sticky notes, where the
students will later upload their brochures or posters.
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
To start the lesson, the students recall the words and ideas from the previous lesson through a short,
puzzle-like game that revises key vocabulary about travel and nature. The students participate in the
gamified vocabulary activity, Destination Decoder. An image is displayed, and the students try to guess
the corresponding word by filling in the missing letters. This activity motivates the students, activates prior
knowledge, and introduces key vocabulary in a fun and interactive way.
While Activity
The students work in their project groups to create a digital brochure or mini presentation about an eco-friendly
destination. Each group selects a destination such as a national park, coral reef, or mountain and designs a
three-page or three-slide digital brochure by using poster and flyer creation tools and interactive presentation
tools. In the first part, the students introduce their chosen destination with pictures and a short description.
In the second part, they write their future holiday plans using the structure “be going to”. In the final part,
the students write short promises and values such as “We promise to protect animals” or “We are going to
be kind to local people.” The students are also reminded to reflect values such as responsibility, kindness,
friendship, cleanliness, and respect in their work.

Post-Activity
Each group presents their digital brochure or mini presentation to the class using the interactive board,
briefly highlighting their future plans and promises as a responsible traveller. After all presentations, the
teacher leads a class reflection by asking questions such as “What did you enjoy most while creating your
brochure?”, “How can we be responsible travellers in real life?”, and “Which value did you practice the most
today?”. The students upload their digital brochures or presentations to the shared digital board, Future
Travel Projects. The teacher explains the homework and shares the Reflective Journal Guideline on EBA, as
the students are assigned to write a reflective journal (Annex-4).
Homework/Assignment
The students write a short, reflective paragraph consisting of five to six sentences. In their writing, the
students are expected to express what they have learnt about being responsible travellers, protecting the
environment, and showing positive values such as respect, kindness, and responsibility. They should also
make connections between the lesson and real-life situations, describing how they can act more responsibly
when travelling or in their daily lives. This task encourages the students to think critically about their learning,
practise using the future form “be going to” in meaningful contexts, and reflect on the importance of caring
for the planet and others (Annex-4).
