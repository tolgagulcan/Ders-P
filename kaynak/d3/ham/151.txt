SURE:3 KOD:
KAV:Habitats, Wild animals, Types of animals
Learning Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an activity titled Guess Game using one of the gamification web tools. The teacher
creates another activity titled Animals using one of the web tools for matching activities. Then, the teacher
designs a video titled Wildlife Safari using one of the digital story creation tools (Annex-1). Lastly, the teacher
creates an activity titled Find the Animal using one of the gamified measurement and evaluation web tools.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher explains the rules of the Guess Game. The students look at the picture and guess the word by
taking turns saying one letter at a time. Each incorrect letter causes one petal to disappear. The activity
focuses on animal vocabulary (e.g. cheetah, bird). After the activity, the teacher asks the students, “Which
animal do you like the most?”, etc. This warm-up task activates prior knowledge, encourages participation,
and helps the students engage with the target vocabulary through a fun and motivating challenge.
While Activity
After the warm-up activity, the teacher moves on to the Animals activity, where the students are asked to
listen carefully and match each sound with the correct picture. This activity helps the students improve their
listening comprehension and vocabulary recognition through sound–image association.
After the matching activity, the teacher continues with the digital story titled Wildlife Safari (Annex-1). In the
story, animals are introduced through simple, descriptive sentences, such as “This is an elephant. It has
big ears. It can walk slowly.” After watching the story, the teacher asks the students both lower-order and
higher-order thinking skills questions to check comprehension and promote deeper thinking. The teacher
first asks factual questions such as “Which animal has big ears?”, “Which animal can run fast?” and “Where
does the giraffe live?” The teacher then continues with higher-order questions such as “Why do you think the
lion is strong?”, “How are the giraffe and the elephant similar or different?” and “If you were an animal in the
Wildlife Safari, which one would you choose and why?” The students share their answers orally, supporting
their ideas with clues from the story. This stage helps the students practise speaking, make comparisons,
justify opinions, and use target vocabulary in meaningful contexts.

Post-Activity
For assessment, the teacher continues with the Find the Animal game. In this game, the students listen to
a short description such as “I have a big mane. I can run fast.” and match it with the correct animal picture.
This task assesses the students’ ability to understand and identify animals based on their physical features
and abilities using can and can’t. The activity reinforces listening comprehension, vocabulary recall, and
sentence-meaning association through an engaging and visual learning process that supports multiple
learning styles.
Framework Visual literacy, Listening /Watching-comprehension, Speaking-expression
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs a word search activity titled Habitats Game using one of the gamification web tools.
The teacher designs an interactive video titled Habitats, including questions, using one of the video editing
tools (Annex-2). The teacher designs an activity titled Wildlife Explorer using one of the web tools for matching
activities.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces the Habitats Game, an interactive word search activity. The students examine
the pictures and try to find hidden words such as forest, savannah, river, and mountain in the letter grid
as quickly as possible. This activity engages the students in an enjoyable challenge, helps them recall key
habitat vocabulary, and prepares them for the lesson with an energetic and focused start.
While Activity
Following the game, the teacher moves on to the Habitats interactive video, where the students are asked
to watch a video about different types of habitats carefully (Annex-2). At the end of the story, the students
answer three interactive questions related to the animals and their habitats. After the activity, the teacher
asks follow-up questions, such as “Which habitat did you like the most?” or “Why do you think animals live
in different habitats?”, to encourage reflection and discussion.
Following the digital story, the teacher asks the students to create a simple digital poster about an animal
and its habitat. Working individually or in pairs, the students choose one animal and identify its natural
habitat. Using a poster and flyer creation tool, they include the animal’s name, its habitat, and one or two
short descriptive sentences (e.g. “This is a polar bear. It lives in the Arctic.”). The teacher monitors the activity,
provides linguistic and technical support, and encourages the use of target vocabulary related to animals
and habitats. This activity reinforces comprehension of the video and supports meaningful language
production in a technology-enhanced learning environment.

Post-Activity
The teacher moves on to the Wildlife Explorer activity, which is a group sort task used for assessment. The
teacher asks the students to drag and drop each item into its correct group. In this activity, the students are
expected to place the pictures related to habitats, wild animals, and types of animals into the appropriate
categories. This task helps the students reinforce their understanding through visual classification and
promotes critical thinking skills by encouraging them to make meaningful connections between concepts.

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a game titled Secret Animal using one of the gamification web tools. The teacher also
develops an activity titled The Jungle Olympics using one of the animation creation tools (Annex-3). The
teacher designs an activity titled Body Parts of Animals using one of the web tools for matching activities.
The teacher also prepares a task titled Animal Name using one of the web tools for short-answer questions.
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces the Secret Animal activity. In this activity, the students try to guess a hidden image.
As parts of the image are revealed, they identify the animal using target vocabulary (e.g. tiger, parrot, lion)
and type their answers into the interactive tool. This activity creates excitement, activates the students’
prior knowledge of animal names, and prepares them for the lesson in an interactive and engaging way.
While Activity
Following the warm-up activity, the teacher continues with The Jungle Olympics animation, where the
students watch an animation about animals competing in different races such as “The Size Race, The Speed
Race, and The Power Race” (Annex-3).The students listen carefully to the sentences describing the results
and focus on the use of comparative and superlative adjectives (e.g. “Cheetah is the fastest,” “Elephant is
bigger than Fox”).After watching the animation, the teacher asks follow-up questions such as “Which race
did you enjoy the most?” or “Which animal surprised you the most?” to encourage reflection, comparison-
making, and speaking practice.
To promote deeper thinking, the teacher also asks higher-order questions such as “Why do you think some
animals are faster or stronger than others in nature?” and “If you could add a new race to The Jungle
Olympics, what would it be, and which animal would win? Why?” Through these questions, the students are
encouraged to think critically, use target language structures, and express their own ideas creatively.

Post-Activity
For assessment, the teacher continues with the Body Parts of Animals activity, where the students listen to
short sentences and drag the recorder icon to the correct body parts on a labelled diagram (e.g. “This is the
feather of a parrot,” “This is the fin of a dolphin”).Through this task, the students match spoken descriptions
with visual body-part images, reinforcing both listening skills and vocabulary knowledge in an interactive way.
Following this activity, the teacher moves on to the Animal Name task. In this activity, the students look at
two animal pictures, read short descriptive sentences, and write the correct animal’s name in the blank. This
task helps the students connect written descriptions with visual clues and reinforces animal vocabulary
through reading and writing practice.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher asks the students to complete an assignment called “Wild Animal Video Project”. In this task, the
students prepare a short video recording about a wild animal of their choice. They describe the animal’s
habitat, colour, body parts, and what it can or can’t do. For example, they can say: “This is a lion. It lives in the
savannah. It has a long tail and sharp teeth. It can run fast, but it can’t climb trees.” The students should also
show a picture or drawing of the animal while speaking. The video should be about 30–45 seconds long, and
the students should speak clearly and confidently in English. After completing the task, the students share
their videos on EBA and write feedback and comments on their classmates’ projects before the next lesson.
