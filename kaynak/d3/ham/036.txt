Activities
Before the Lesson
The students are required to complete Vocabulary Exercise 1: On the Phone on EBA, where they match
commonly used phone conversation phrases with their corresponding images. The teacher creates
an interactive game titled What’s Behind Game using a gamified measurement and evaluation web
tool. Then, the teacher prepares an activity titled Ways of Communication using one of the web tools
for matching activities. Additionally, the teacher develops a digital story titled Plans on the Phone
using a digital story creation tool (Annex-1). The teacher prepares an exercise named Listen & Find
using a web tool for fill-in-the-blank questions.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
To warm up, the teacher tells the students that they are going to play the What’s Behind Game. In this
game, students move the cursor across the screen to reveal a hidden image. This interactive task
captures the students’ attention and helps them to draw on their existing knowledge of the topic.
While Activity
The teacher incorporates the Ways of Communication exercise, in which the students listen to audio
clips and match each form of communication to the relevant picture. This activity improves the
students’ auditory comprehension and reinforces their understanding of different communication
methods.
Following the matching exercise, the teacher moves on to the digital story titled Plans on the Phone,
which features an audio recording of a phone conversation between two friends discussing their
weekend plans (Annex-1). Through this story, the students are introduced to everyday expressions
related to making plans, offering help, and refusing politely. After listening, the teacher checks the
students’ comprehension by asking questions such as, “What does James want to do?” and “Why can’t
Leo play video games right now?”, helping them to recall and reflect on the conversation’s content.
To further encourage critical thinking and student engagement, the teacher may also ask questions
such as, “Do you agree with Leo’s decision? Why or why not?’ This encourages students to express their
own opinions and back them up with logical reasoning.
Following the discussion, the teacher divides the class into pairs or small groups and invites each
group to create a similar conversation on the phone. These conversations should include common
phrases for arranging and rearranging plans, as well as references to school responsibilities and
leisure activities. Once they have finished, each group performs their conversation for the class.

Post-Activity
The teacher introduces the Listen & Find activity, which features a message text exchange between
two friends. In this activity, the students listen to audio clips containing six missing words or phrases
from the message. The transcript is displayed with gaps, and the students listen carefully to identify the
correct words. They then complete the task by dragging and dropping the audio-based words onto the
correct gaps in the message.
Once the activity has been completed, the teacher reviews the answers with the class and asks
comprehension questions to reinforce understanding. These may include questions such as “Why can’t
Mary join the walk?” or “What is planned for Thursday?”. This activity develops students’ listening and
word recognition skills, as well as promoting attention to detail and the use of language in context.
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a memory card game titled Find the Pairs using a gamification web tool to reinforce
vocabulary through an engaging and interactive learning experience. In addition to this, the teacher
designs an activity named Courier Call using a digital story creation tool (Annex-2). Furthermore, the
teacher prepares another activity titled Ways We Connect using one of the web tools for short answer
questions.
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
At the beginning of the lesson, the teacher uses the Find the Pairs memory card game to activate the
students’ prior knowledge and promote active engagement. This interactive task requires the students
to recall the positions of various cards and accurately match each pair. The students are encouraged
to complete the game efficiently and aim to surpass their previous performance. By incorporating
elements of game-based learning, the activity supports vocabulary retention, enhances short-term
memory, and fosters digital literacy skills.
While Activity
Following the memory card game, the teacher moves on to the Courier Call digital story (Annex-2).
In this activity, the students listen to a conversation between a courier and a customer, then answer
questions about it, such as “What is the address the courier confirms?” and “When will the courier
arrive?”. The teacher also asks the students to identify phrases used to confirm information, such as “Is
it 45 Elm Street, Apartment 3B?”. The students can then practise the conversation in pairs, taking turns
to be the courier and the customer. To make the activity more varied, the teacher could ask students
to create a new version of the conversation by changing details such as the address or delivery time.
This helps them practise being flexible in their use of language and reinforces their vocabulary.

Post-Activity
As a part of the assessment, the teacher integrates the Ways We Connect activity, which features
vocabulary items such as mobile phone, telephone, letter, and e-mail. The students are encouraged
to observe the pictures and type the corresponding words that represent different communication
tools. Each word begins with a given first letter to help with vocabulary recall. The activity focuses
on writing skills, helping students to reinforce their understanding of communication methods and
practise correct spelling in a fun, interactive way.
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs an exercise named Find the Double using one of the gamification web tools.
Subsequently, to enhance student engagement, the teacher creates an animation titled Customer
Service Call using one of the animation creation tools (Annex-3). In addition, the teacher prepares
another activity titled Caller or Receiver using a web tool for matching exercises (Annex-4).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher uses the Find the Double exercise as a warm-up activity, prompting the students to quickly
identify the repeated image. This engaging task enhances the students' visual recognition skills and
reinforces vocabulary retention.
While Activity
Following the warm-up activity, the teacher moves on to the Customer Service Call animation
(Annex-3). In this activity, the students listen to a conversation between a customer and a service
representative, then answer comprehension questions such as “Why is Mary calling?” or “What solution
does the representative suggest?” to check their understanding of the conversation. The teacher also
highlights key expressions used for reporting a problem and offering assistance, such as “I’m calling
because…” and “You can try restarting your modem.”
After the story, the teacher organises a Communication Methods Station Activity, dividing the
classroom into four stations, each representing a different communication method:..........Phone Call,
@
..........Text Message, ..........Email, and ..........Video Call. The students move to the station that matches the
communication method they want to practise, where they complete simple tasks. In the Phone Call
station, the students practise a conversation about weekend plans, while in the Text Message station,
they write about what they are doing today. At the Email station, they write a short mail to a teacher
about homework, and at the Video Call station, they act out a conversation about their favourite
hobby. After the tasks are completed, the students move to different stations. Some volunteers share
their responses with the class. This activity lets students practise different ways of communicating,
improve their language skills, and understand how to use language in real-life situations.

Post-Activity
For the assessment, the students move on to the Caller or Receiver categorisation task (Annex-4). In
this interactive drag-and-drop exercise, the students are invited to read sentences that appear on
the screen and decide whether each sentence would typically be said by a caller or a receiver during
a phone conversation. They then drag and drop each sentence into the appropriate category. For
example, the phrase »Please hold on the line.« falls under the receiver category. This activity supports
the development of functional language and helps students to distinguish between roles in everyday
conversations while reinforcing comprehension.
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates an exercise titled Communication Bowling using a gamified measurement and
assessment web tool to make learning more engaging (Annex-5). Then, the teacher designs another
exercise named Match & Learn exercise using one of the web tools for matching activities. The teacher
prepares a video titled Reading Time with a video editing tool and makes the content interactive
by embedding questions within it. Finally, the teacher develops a Phone Quiz utilising a web tool for
multiple-choice questions (Annex-6).
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher uses the Communication Bowling exercise as a warm-up activity, in which the students
roll a ball towards a set of pins, each labelled with different forms of communication (e.g. phone call,
video call) (Annex-5). If the student answers correctly, the pin falls and they earn a point. If they are
unsure, the ball rolls past the pin without knocking it down and it is the next student's turn. This activity
promotes visual recognition and reinforces vocabulary retention in an interactive and fun way.
While Activity
The teacher uses the Match & Learn activity. In this exercise, the students match each word with its
correct definition. They read the words and select the correct definition. This activity helps the students
to learn new vocabulary and understand words better in a fun and interactive way.
Following the matching activity, the teacher moves on to the Reading Time activity. In this activity, the
students are asked to read a series of short telephone conversations either individually or in pairs.
Each conversation reflects a real-life situation, such as arranging appointments, rearranging plans or
verifying information. After reading each dialogue, students answer a related comprehension question
designed to assess their understanding of the key details and intentions within the conversation. This
activity reinforces the use of functional language and supports reading comprehension and critical
thinking in a communicative context.

Phone Time
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
After the activity, the students complete a digital task to reinforce their understanding and practise
real-life communication skills. For this task, they work either individually or in pairs to create a short
telephone conversation based on a real-life situation, such as arranging an appointment, confirming
a reservation or rearranging a meeting. The students use a digital tool to present their conversations,
and they are encouraged to add visuals, emojis, or sound effects to make their presentations more
engaging. Finally, they share their digital dialogues on a shared platform and comment on each
other’s work. This task enhances students’ speaking and writing skills, as well as fostering creativity,
collaboration and digital literacy.
Post-Activity
The teacher assesses the students by administering the Phone Quiz, which is a multiple-choice
activity in which students must examine visuals and read the accompanying sentences or short texts
carefully before selecting the correct option that corresponds to the description provided (Annex-6).
This activity helps students to develop their reading comprehension and vocabulary skills, as well as
their ability to connect visual information with written language.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher sets the students the task of creating a recorded phone conversation in pairs. Each pair
will have a real phone conversation about a chosen topic, such as arranging to meet up, discussing
school projects, sharing personal news, or asking for help. Throughout the conversation, both students
are expected to use common phone expressions and polite language. They should prepare by noting
down key phrases, including greetings, questions and farewells. They will then call each other and have
a 2–3 minutes conversation. Once the conversation has been recorded, the students will submit the
audio file via the designated classroom management tool. This assignment will help students practise
phone-related expressions, improve their pronunciation and fluency, and build their confidence in
using English in real-life communication situations.
