Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher uses one of the web tools for instant feedback to assess the students’ prior knowledge.
The teacher prepares a Buildings in My Neighbourhood vocabulary presentation consisting of public
buildings (Annex-1).
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces the concept of “public buildings” as those accessible to all. The teacher shares
a link for an activity prepared by a web tool for instant feedback. The students list the types of public
buildings they are familiar with, entering their responses directly into the tool. This collaborative
activity allows the students to share ideas and learn from each other, while the teacher can assess
their understanding and provide guidance.

While Activity
The teacher initiates the Buildings in My Neighbourhood vocabulary presentation consisting of
several public buildings (Annex-1). Following the presentation, the students are informed that they
will participate in a brief, interactive activity. The teacher requests that the students take notes on
the public buildings that were presented. The individual with the most accurate responses will be
declared the winner. The teacher then poses questions to the students to assess their comprehension
and encourages them to discuss the material. Sample questions include, “Which buildings did you
see?”, “What is the purpose of people going there?” and “How often do you visit such places?” The
teacher monitors the students’ comprehension.
Post-Activity
To reinforce vocabulary related to public buildings and the use of “to” for expressing purpose, the
teacher divides the class into teams using a tool for creating digital boards or post-its. The teacher
provides each team with clues or riddles about different public buildings. The teams should search
online for images of buildings that match the given clues and share them in their designated area.
Once a team identifies a building, the teacher asks them to explain why they would visit it using
the phrase “to...,” for example, “We go to the bank to draw money.” The teacher displays the tool for
creating digital boards or post-its on the interactive whiteboard to ensure all groups’ responses are
visible. The lesson concludes with a review of all responses, followed by necessary corrections.
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares Buildings Flashcards of the public buildings using Visual creation and editing
tools and prints them before the lesson. The teacher prepares Bingo Cards with pictures of public
buildings sing visual creation and editing tools (Annex-2).
2. Ders ( Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the flashcards and wants the students to guess the names of the buildings.
The teacher asks the students to discuss reasons why people visit these buildings. Then the teacher
introduces, or reviews vocabulary related to public buildings and the purposes for visiting these
buildings.
While Activity
The teacher divides the students into small groups and assigns each group a scenario where they
have to visit a public building (e.g. planning a visit to a museum, or seeking information at a library).
The students discuss and describe the building, including the purpose for visiting, to their partner for
a while with their team. Then they role-play the scenario, using the vocabulary and phrases related
to the purpose of the building. Throughout the role-play, the teacher listens to student dialogues to
assess their understanding and use of relevant vocabulary and phrases.

Post-Activity
At the end of the lesson, the students play a game of Bingo. The teacher explains the rules of the
game. The teacher distributes the Bingo Cards prepared before the lesson to the students and then
calls out the names of the buildings (Annex-2). The students mark the pictures of the buildings they
hear on their cards. The first student to mark all the squares on their card and shout ‘Bingo!’ wins the
game.
Homework/Assignment
The teacher requires the students to visit at least three different public buildings (such as the library,
post office, park, or museum) and maintain a diary of their visits, providing a description of the
buildings and the rationale behind their choice of destination. The students engage in reflection on
their experiences and the insights gained. They present the work they have prepared in EBA groups.
The teacher asks the students to complete Vocabulary Exercise 1: Public Buildings and Vocabulary
Exercise 2: Public Buildings as an assignment to reinforce the theme and the new words in the unit.
Before the Lesson
The teacher brings printed building flashcards. The teacher prepares a Places in the City worksheet.
The worksheet contains pictures of public buildings and boxes beneath them where the students
can write the corresponding names.
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces a riddle game to the class. The teacher will read a riddle about a public
building: “I have a vast collection of books and stories, yet I am unable to read or recount them.
What am I ?” The students will attempt to guess the answer. The first student to correctly identify the
answer will earn a point. The winner will be declared at the end of the game.
While Activity
As a whole class, the students briefly discuss the function of each public building. The teacher uses
simple explanations and gestures to aid understanding. Then the class collaboratively creates create
a reading text and comprehension check questions via an AI powered tool by using the keywords
in the unit. They can use prompts like, “Write a brief summary of Sarah’s day, including her visits
to the library, bank, and post office and prepare comprehension checking questions.” The teacher
introduces the reading passage that describes a visit to different public buildings. The students
read the passage aloud. Subsequently, the students are required to respond to a series of questions
designed to assess their level of comprehension.

Post-Activity
When the students answer the questions, the teacher distributes the Places in the City worksheet
prepared beforehand. The worksheet features images of public buildings and empty spaces for the
students to write their names. The students may complete the worksheet individually or in pairs. The
students may engage in peer review by exchanging worksheets and providing feedback to their
classmates. Such activities can facilitate the development of critical thinking and communication
skills.
Before the Lesson
Prior to the lesson, the teacher selects a public building and utilises an AI-powered tool to create
a descriptive paragraph. A peer assessment form has been designed for the students to evaluate
their peers’ written work (Annex-3). Furthermore, a self-assessment form has been designed for the
students to evaluate their own work (Annex-4).
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates a memory game. The students are provided with a list of public buildings and
then they close their eyes and attempt to identify the one that is missing.
While Activity
The teacher selects a public building and composes a descriptive paragraph using an AI-powered
tool. The prompts can be used as follows: “Write a descriptive paragraph about the local library.”
Once the paragraph has been created and read, the class engages in a discussion about the key
elements of the paragraph, including the building’s appearance, purpose, and reasons for its appeal.
The teacher requests that the students select one building and compose a descriptive paragraph
about it. Subsequently, the students share their written work via a digital platform, creating digital
boards or post-its after they complete their writings. The students dedicate time to reading each
other’s written work. The teacher encourages constructive feedback and the students evaluate each
other’s writings on clarity, organisation, and descriptive language (Annex-3).
Post-Activity
The students are required to revise their paragraphs in accordance with the feedback received. They
must then produce a final draft of their descriptive paragraphs on a public building of their choice.
The writing pieces with the most positive comments are selected for reading aloud to the class.
Finally, the students are asked to provide a self-assessment of the knowledge gained during the
lesson (Annex-4).
Homework/Assignment
The teacher assigns Vocabulary Exercise 3: Public Buildings and Grammar Exercise 4: Public Buildings
on EBA to the students and wants them to complete these exercises.
