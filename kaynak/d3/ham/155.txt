Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to complete the Vocabulary Exercise 1: My Daily Routine on EBA. In this
activity, the students match words related to routines with the images representing those routines.
The teacher then designs a KWL Chart using one of the visual creation and editing tools and prints
it out. Next, the teacher creates a video titled Tom’s Routines using one of the video editing tools
(Annex-1). Finally, for the assessment, the teacher prepares a game named Catch the Routine using
one of the game-based measurement and evaluation web tools.
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher begins the lesson by asking the students what their favourite routine is and then invites
them to consider the importance of routines in their lives. They ask the students to pay close attention
to the relationship between routines and success. After this discussion, the teacher displays the KWL
Chart on the interactive whiteboard. They then distribute copies of the chart and ask the students
to pair up with a nearby classmate, focusing exclusively on the ‘K’ and ‘W’ sections and completing
these parts of the chart.
While Activity
Following the sequence outlined in the chart, the teacher proceeds to the video titled Tom’s Routines,
in which the students identify simple written texts about daily routines (Annex-1). The teacher uses the
video as an introductory resource to prepare the students for the subsequent discussion and capture
their attention. The students are then invited to work in pairs and discuss the following questions:
‘What time do you get up?’, ‘What do you do after school?’, and ‘What are your evening routines?’. They
then compare Tom’s routines with their own to identify similarities and differences. This activity aims
to cultivate critical thinking, active listening and structured speaking in a captivating manner. The
teacher provides ongoing feedback to support the students’ engagement and progress throughout
the activity.
Post-Activity
Following the activity, to assess vocabulary acquisition, the teacher implements the Catch the Routine
game, wherein the students match the visuals with the corresponding daily routines. Finally, the
students are asked to work in pairs, focusing on the ‘L’ segment of the chart. They are then required to
complete and evaluate the chart, reflecting on what they have learnt.

Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to complete the Reading Exercise: A Friend from Turkey on EBA. For
this activity, the students read the text and answer the multiple-choice questions. Meanwhile, the
teacher searches for videos depicting the daily routines of successful individuals in science or sports.
Then the teacher creates a visual titled Tips for Success using one of the visual creation and editing
tools before printing it out.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher uses a short video presentation to illustrate the daily routine of a well-known scientist
or a successful athlete to the students. The students are then tasked with identifying the common
elements in the routines of successful individuals.
While Activity
The teacher presents the visual aid titled Tips for Success, which will be used in the jigsaw technique of
the cooperative learning approach. The students are then assigned numbers from 1 to 5, which are used
to form groups. The students then convene in groups, discuss the relationship between the routines
and success and focus on the titles such as sleeping time, eating habits, and free time activities for
a short time. Subsequently, the students with identical numbers are grouped into an ‘expert group’,
tasked with investigating and deliberating the significance of the assigned titles in the context of
success. The teacher informs the students that the research findings would be utilised in the creation
of an animation during the subsequent lesson. The expert groups may utilise artificial intelligence-
powered tools or chatbots to conduct more in-depth searches, and they are also expected to take
notes. The teacher provides ongoing feedback to facilitate the students’ engagement and progress
throughout the activity.
Post-Activity
Once they have completed their assigned task, the expert groups will return to their home groups.
Each student is expected to share their findings, ensuring that all group members are exposed to
every topic and developing their critical thinking skills. Afterwards, there will be a discussion about the
important role that these skills and routines play in the personal development of successful individuals.

A Day in My Life
Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a digital board titled Use Your Imagination, using one of the tools for creating
digital boards or post-its.
3. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The students continue to work in the same groups as in the previous lesson. The teacher uses an online
discussion tool and starts the lesson by posing the following questions: ‘If you have a chance, who do
you want to be for one day?’ and ‘Why?’. After collecting the students’ responses, the teacher tells
them that they will play a game titled Mimicry Play Scenario: ‘A Day in Another’s Shoes’. The teacher
applies the game-based learning approach, in which the students create the animation of a daily
routine and act it out. This strategy is designed to enhance the students' imagination, collaboration,
communication and creativity.
While Activity
The teacher shares the Use Your Imagination digital board link and invites the students to select a
character, either real or imaginary. Working together, the students are tasked with creating an avatar
to represent their chosen character, as well as an animation to illustrate a typical day in the life of that
character, using one of the animation creation tools. They can also voice their animations by narrating
or performing dialogue for their characters. Each group member must take responsibility for a specific
aspect of the animation, as they will be tasked with enacting it. For example, one student could be
assigned the role of the teacher or the character's mother. The animation could depict a typical day at
school, work or home. The students share the animation links on the digital board. The teacher provides
ongoing feedback to engage and support the students throughout the activity.
Post-Activity
The students watch animations and comment on their friends' work, sharing their opinions. Once all
the animations have been discussed, the teacher summarises the key points about the relationship
between routines and success, raising the students' awareness in the process.
Homework/Assignment
The students are tasked with performing their animation script, recording it, and sharing the recording
on the digital board. Each student is expected either to provide a positive comment on their peers'
work, or to offer suggestions.
