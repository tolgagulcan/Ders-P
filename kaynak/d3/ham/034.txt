Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates two mind maps: Good Personal Traits and Bad Personal Traits leveraging a mind
mapping tool to stimulate engagement during the warm-up activity. Then the teacher creates a digital
story titled Two Close Friends: Jane and Mary utilising a digital story creation tool to illustrate making
suggestions/offers, accepting or refusing an offer (Annex-1). Lastly, to check reading comprehension, the
teacher develops Multiple-Choice Questions using a web tool for multiple-choice questions (Annex-2).
1. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher writes “My Best Friend” on the board and says “My best friend is Büşra. I get on well with her
because she is kind and thoughtful.” Then the teacher asks the students to make groups of four. The
teacher displays Good Personal Traits such as loyal, generous, helpful, caring and Bad Personal Traits
such as jealous, sneaky, unreliable on the interactive whiteboard and wants the students to look at the
mind maps and to form sentences to describe their friend. The students are invited to provide examples
of either positive or negative personal traits. The teacher provides an illustrative example on the board
to guide the students: “My best friend, Büşra, is very helpful and generous. She is a bit jealous, but I
love her very much.” Each group member considers the characteristics of their teammates and then
presents their findings to the class.

While Activity
The teacher initiates the digital story titled Two Close Friends: Jane and Mary (Annex-1). Before starting
to read the story, the teacher writes the structures of making suggestions/offers and the structures of
accepting/refusing an offer on the board to remind the structures to the students. The teacher asks
the students to take note of the structures they see in the story. The teacher informs the students that
the multiple-choice questions are related to the story, so the students should follow the story carefully.
While reading the story, in addition to the structures, the teacher emphasises the adjectives describing
a friend. As the teacher narrates the story during its creation, the narration can also be repurposed for
the listening activity.
Post-Activity
The teacher shares the link of Multiple-Choice Questions with the students and states that the activity
will be in a game form (Annex-2). The student who provides the most accurate response will be declared
the winner of the game. The teacher encourages the students to feel free to take an active role in
the game and says”Good job!” when they find the correct answer. With the help of the gamification
approach the students not only learn but also enjoy the learning process.
Before the Lesson
The teacher writes the cards with three good personal traits and three bad personal traits and shuffles
the cards. Additionally, the teacher prepares a guideline for the digital story writing project and makes
copies to distribute to the students (Annex-3). Lastly, the teacher prepares a group evaluation form for
the digital story writing activity and makes copies for the groups and informs the students in advance
(Annex-4).
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher prepares a set of cards, each displaying a positive personal trait and a negative personal
trait. The cards are then shuffled and distributed to the students at random. The teacher asks the
students to group with peers holding cards featuring the same adjective. In this way the class will be
divided into six groups randomly and the curiosity about their groups will refresh the students before
the planned activity.
While Activity
The teacher hands out the copies of the guideline for the digital story project and reminds the students
how to use a digital story creation tool (Annex-3). The teacher asks the students to write a story about
“Friendship” and not to forget to use both good and bad personal traits. Moreover, the teacher wants
the students to make offers and accept or refuse the offers. In addition to incorporating visuals into their
digital story, the students must record and add their own audio narration. In this way, both the creativity
and the reading skills of the students will be improved. The teacher reminds the students that their
performance will be evaluated by their group members so, each student needs to take an active role
in creating the story (Annex-4).

Before the Lesson
The teacher creates an Invitation Card with a visual creation tool, showcasing its essential components
(Annex-5). Additionally, the teacher prepares a Matching Activity using a web tool for matching activities
(Annex-6).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
As a warm-up activity the teacher initiates “Four Corners of Me” activity. To tell the details of the activity,
the teacher writes “Ann” in the middle of the whiteboard and after writing “Four Corners of Ann”, draws
four different boxes and writes four different details about Ann’s life. For example, Ann’ birthday, Ann’s
favourite food, Ann’s favourite place, one thing positive about Ann’s character. The teacher asks the
students to take a piece of a paper and to write four corners of themselves. Once the four corners
have been completed, the students exchange their answers with their classmates and engage in a
discussion about them. Once the discussion has concluded, the teacher requests that the students
keep their friend’s birthday in mind.
While Activity
The teacher displays the Invitation Card for a birthday party (Annex-5). The teacher focuses on the
necessary elements in an invitation card such as the event, the date/time, the place and the contact
person. The teacher reads the invitation card in detail and to check the comprehension, asks the
questions like “What is the event?”, “When is the birthday party?”, “Where is the birthday party?” and “Who
is the contact person?” Then the teacher writes the questions on the board and asks the students to
note these questions. Moreover, the teacher reminds the students that they will write a response to this
invitation card in the next lesson.
Post-Activity
The teacher initiates a discussion on the Matching Activity to assess reading comprehension (Annex-6).
Each student participates in pairs, taking turns to ask and answer questions. Then they proceed to the
interactive whiteboard to match questions with answers.
Before the Lesson
The teacher shares the Invitation Card on EBA as the students will write a response to this invitation
card (Annex-5).

4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
To increase the energy and enthusiasm of the students, the teacher starts the lesson with “Three Truths
and a Lie” activity. Each student writes their name and four personal traits (good or bad) on a piece of
paper. Three true personal traits and one lie. Then the students walk around the classroom and find their
pairs. The students show their paper to each other and try to guess which personal trait is a lie.
While Activity
The teacher revisits the Invitation Card, reviewing its elements to reinforce understanding among
students (Annex-5). The teacher assigns the students to write a response to the invitation card by using
a visual creation and editing tool. The students may accept or refuse the invitation. If the invitation
is accepted, the students are expected to request further details in their response. Conversely, if the
students refuse, they are required to provide a valid reason. When the students complete their writing
task, they read their responses to the classmates and the teacher provides feedback to the students.
Post-Activity
To go over the making offers, accepting, and refusing structures and to determine the level of the
students’ comprehension, the teacher initiates Listening Exercise: New Friends on EBA. The students
listen to the activity and then fill in the blanks with the appropriate structures.
