Activities
Before the Lesson
First, the teacher designs a word cloud named TV Programmes using one of the word cloud creation
tools. The teacher then creates a presentation titled Types of TV Programmes using one of the
interactive presentation tools. Finally, for the assessment, the teacher prepares a Reflective Journal
using one of the visual creation and editing tools and prints it out (Annex-1).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher commences the lesson by posing the questions, ‘Do you like watching TV?’, and ‘What kind
of Tv programmes do you watch, and why?’ and subsequently invites the students to contemplate
the significance of watching appropriate programmes for their age. After the discussion, the teacher
displays the TV Programmes word cloud on the interactive whiteboard to illustrate the concept,
encouraging the students to select their favourite programme and construct a sentence around
it. Finally, the class collectively creates an additional word cloud incorporating television-related
vocabulary using one of the word cloud creation tools.
While Activity
Following the word cloud activity, the teacher proceeds to the presentation titled Types of TV
Programmes, in which the students identify different types of TV programmes and TV signs. The
teacher uses the presentation to introduce the topic and stimulate the students’ curiosity ahead of
the main activity. The teacher initiates group work by inviting the students to team up and explore the
following questions together: ‘What kind of programmes do you prefer?’, ‘Why is it important to watch
programmes that are appropriate for our age?’, ‘Does watching too much TV affect our creativity?’,
‘What effect do TV programmes have on our emotions?’, ‘Why do people from different countries or
cultures prefer different types of TV programmes?’, and ‘If you could create your own TV channel for
young people, what programmes would you include?’ This activity aims to cultivate critical thinking,
active listening and structured speaking, and the teacher provides ongoing feedback to facilitate
student engagement and progress.
Post-Activity
After the discussing TV viewing habits, the teacher moves on to the assessment part and hands out
copies of the Reflective Journal, in which the students are required to answer the questions reflecting
on their TV viewing habits. (Annex-1).

Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares a visual titled Six Corners of Watching TV using one of the visual creation and
editing tools and prints it out. Then the teacher creates a digital board titled Share Your Insights using
one of the tools for creating digital boards or post-its.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the Six Corners of Watching TV visual which includes categories such as
documentaries, cartoons and sitcoms, on the interactive whiteboard. The teacher then divides the
printed version of the visual into six sections, which are affixed to the six corners of the classroom.
Subsequently, the teacher invites the students to examine the visual on the board and asks, “What
kind of programmes do you watch the most?” The students are then encouraged to move to the
corresponding corner where they watch TV the most, write their names on the visual, and discuss
with the other students why they watch these programmes.
While Activity
The teacher then divides the class into six groups, using the names written on the six corners of
watching TV visuals. The teacher shares the link of the Share Your Insights digital board with the
groups. The students who watch similar programmes on TV convene to create sticky notes to paste
on the digital board. Using the Six Thinking Hats technique of the active learning approach, the
teacher distributes the topics that the students need to research. The White Hat group uses chatbots
or search engines to find facts about the effects of TV programmes on audiences and writes the key
points on the digital board ‘Documentaries increase our knowledge’. The Red Hat group uses one of
the visual creation and editing tools to create short emoji or mood boards representing their feelings
about different types of TV programmes, to which they add simple sentences: 'I feel excited while
watching quiz shows' and then share the visuals on the digital board. The Black Hat group uses one of
the visual creation and editing tools to create a cause-and-effect visual showing the negative effects
of negative examples on TV: 'Horror and war films Violence increases Empathy decreases'. The
Yellow Hat group designs a digital poster using a poster and flyer creation tool to show the benefits of
watching educational programmes. They use slogans such as 'Learning Right from Your Living Room!’.
The Green Hat group prepares a cartoon video using one of the video editing tools, adding creative
solutions such as 'Teaching Numbers with Animals'. The Blue Hat group collects all the group's work
on the digital board and presents a summary of ideas about the positive and negative effects of
TV programmes on the interactive board. 'Pay attention to what you watch, because you turn into
what you see on TV.' As the lesson draws to a close, the class will have created a collective product
through their collaborative efforts. The students contribute to this collective endeavour within the
larger group while also working in smaller groups. It is emphasised that the results of the research will
be presented to the class, with the teacher offering guidance as required.

Before the Lesson
First the teacher prepares What Do You Prefer? visuals using one of the visual creation and editing tools
and prints them out. The teacher then creates a presentation titled TV Viewing Habits using one of the
interactive presentation tools (Annex-2). Finally, for the assessment, the teacher designs Chat Cards
using one of the gamified measurement and evaluation web tools (Annex-3).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the What Do You Prefer? visuals, which include talk shows, news programmes and
documentaries, on the interactive whiteboard. In this exercise, students work in pairs to identify their
partners' favourite TV programmes. They are also required to describe their TV viewing habits, using
frequency adverbs. For example, 'I always watch quiz shows on Friday nights, but I never watch soap
operas'.
While Activity
Following the discussion activity, the teacher proceeds to the presentation titled TV Viewing Habits,
in which the students identify the expressions and phrases related to daily routines and preferences
(Annex-2). The teacher periodically pauses the presentation and poses questions such as “What kind of
programmes does your father watch?”, “Do you like watching the news?”, “How often does your mother
watch sports programmes?” and “What did you learn about your classmates' TV viewing habits?”, with
the aim of creating an interactive learning environment and encouraging the students to express
themselves.
Post-Activity
Following the preceding presentation, to assess the comprehension, the teacher initiates the Chat
Cards where the students are requested to answer the questions, with guidance offered by the teacher
as required (Annex-3).

4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
Using one of the online discussion tools the teacher initiates the lesson with a discussion. The students
are required to provide swift responses to the scenarios provided such as, “Your grandmother wants
to watch an Indian soap opera, but you can’t stand watching soap operas. What do you do?”, “It is your
turn to watch the TV, but your sister/brother insists on watching the same cartoon for several times.
What do you do?”. Once all the scenarios have been discussed, the teacher summarises the key points
about the subject.
While Activity
Following the discussion, the students are assigned to collaborate in groups of four in order to prepare
a presentation using one of the interactive presentation tools to describe the TV habits of their family
members. The students are required to use the frequency adverbs when describing the routines and
phrases to state preferences. The objective of this activity is to cultivate creativity and productivity
in a captivating manner while using daily routines and preferences. The teacher provides ongoing
feedback to support the students' engagement and progress throughout the activity.
Post-Activity
Once all groups have finished designing their presentations, they share their work on EBA. The teacher
and the students then analyse the presentations in detail and provide feedback.
Homework/Assignment
The students are assigned the task of completing the Reading Exercise: The Good and Bad Sides of TV
on EBA. The students are required to read the passage and answer the questions.
