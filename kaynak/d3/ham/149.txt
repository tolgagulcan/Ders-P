Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher is required to prepare various digital tools to enhance the student engagement and
learning. First, the class is divided into two teams for a memory game, where students practise
recalling names and personal information, fostering language skills and a positive classroom
environment. Following this, an activity titled Countries is prepared using web tools for matching
activities, allowing the students to connect countries with their corresponding flags (Annex-1). Next
a digital story titled My Friends Around the World is created using digital story creation tools, helping
students to explore different cultures and preferences (Annex-2). Finally, the lesson concludes with
comprehension questions to assess the students’ understanding of the story. The teacher designs
Multiple-Choice Questions using a web tool for multiple-choice questions (Annex-3).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher introduces a memory game designed to enhance student engagement and recall of
names and personal information. The class is divided into two teams. Upbeat music plays while the
students pass a soft ball around the circle. When the music stops, the student holding the ball shares
their name and a personal fact (e.g. “My name is Mary, and I love ice cream.”).

Each subsequent student must repeat the previous the student’s name and fact before adding their
own. This process continues around the class, creating a lively atmosphere that encourages active
participation. The memory game helps the students learn names, practise speaking and listening,
and fosters a positive, comfortable classroom environment.
While Activity
Following the memory game, the teacher introduces the Countries matching activity. In this task,
the students are asked to match countries with their corresponding flags (Annex-1). After the flag
matching activity, the teacher introduces the digital story My Friends Around the World (Annex-2). The
students improve their reading skills and enhance their awareness of cultural differences, personal
preferences and cultural interactions while reading the story.
Post-Activity
Following the digital story activity, the teacher moves on to comprehension questions. These Multiple-
Choice Questions are directly related to “My Friends Around the World” story, helping the students
check their understanding of the content, including the countries, preferences, and interactions
mentioned in the story (Annex-3).
Before the Lesson
Prior to the class, the teacher is required to create forms using interactive presentation tools: a digital
handout and a photocopy version for the students. The first form is a Find Someone Who exercise
using a digital handout or form created with interactive presentation tools (Annex-4). The second
form is a list of Likes and Dislikes includes various school subjects, hobbies, or activities (e.g. favourite
class, hobby, or food) (Annex-5). These forms are displayed on the interactive whiteboard and also
provided to the students as photocopies.
2. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher presents the Find Someone Who activity in both digital and print formats (Annex-4). The
digital version was displayed on the interactive whiteboard, and printed copies were provided to
the students. The students move around the classroom ,using prompts from the form, such as “Find
someone who likes English” to interact with classmates and complete their worksheets. Each student
can only sign one box on another classmate’s form, promoting interaction among all the students.
To make the activity more playful and high-energy, the students can perform a fun movement
before signing a box, such as doing a quick dance move or doing a high five. After the activity, the
teacher asks the students to share some of the answers they discovered, which helps reinforce the
information and encourages class discussion.

While Activity
Following the activity, the students are asked to pair up with a classmate for a speaking exercise.
Each student should use their ‘Find Someone Who’ form to discuss their likes and dislikes with their
partner. The teacher asks the students to use full sentences to describe their likes and dislikes, such
as “Ali likes English.” or “Elif doesn’t like Math.” They should share what they discovered about their
preferences and dislikes from the form, and then take turns discussing their own likes and dislikes. For
example, one student might say, “I found out that (Name) likes pizza. I also like pizza, but I dislike sushi.
What about you? What do you like and dislike?” This exercise helps the students practise speaking in
a more personal context.
Post-Activity
Following the activity, the students are invited to create an emoji story about themselves. They
introduce themselves and use emojis to represent their likes and dislikes. The teacher displays the
Likes and Dislikes list on the interactive whiteboard (Annex-5). This list includes various school subjects,
hobbies or activities etc. The students choose some likes/dislikes that best reflect their preferences,
like their favourite class, hobby, or food, and write a short sentence about each one, such as “I like
Maths, but I dislike Music.” They then share their emoji stories with their partners or the class.
Before the Lesson
The teacher asks the students to complete Vocabulary Exercise 1: Hello! activity on EBA. Cards with
country names and corresponding nationalities are prepared by the teacher. For the following
activity, the teacher designs an activity titled Mary and Hans using a web tool for fill-in-the-blank
questions (Annex-6). The teacher prepares another activity titled Countries and Nationalities using a
web tool for matching activities and asks the students to listen to the sounds and place the words in
the correct groups. “Dream Passport” templates are prepared by the teacher and distributed to the
students, who are then asked to fill them in according to their own dreams (Annex-7).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher uses cards with the names of countries and nationalities, shuffles them and distributes
them to the students. The class is divided into teams and a race is organised using an hourglass.
Each student runs to the front of the class, chooses a card and runs back to their team to match it.
The next team member can only go after completing the previous task. To enhance the learning
experience, the students may engage in physical challenges such as jumping on one foot or walking
backwards while competing with their peers. Each team matches pairs of countries and nationalities
(e.g.,Japan-Japanese) and shares an interesting fact about the country. The team that matches the
most pairs correctly within the time limit wins. This activity helps students learn about countries and
nationalities while practising matching skills in a fun, active and competitive way.

Technology-Enhanced Learning Scenarios
While Activity
The teacher introduces an exercise with incomplete sentences about Mary and Hans, accompanied
by relevant images (Annex-6). The students are required to complete the sentences based on the
visuals provided. This exercise reinforces their understanding of personal details and preferences
while practicing fill-in-the-blank tasks. In the Countries and Nationalities activity, the teacher plays
an audio recording that mentions various country names and corresponding nationalities. The
students are then required to categorise the words they hear by placing them in the correct group,
either as a country or a nationality.
Post-Activity
The students are given a passport template with sections for different countries (Annex-7). Each
student inscribes their name on the passport and selects a country they aspire to visit. They research
their country, gathering information on notable traditions, landmarks, and heritage. They then fill out
their passports with the information they gathered. Each student presents their completed passport
to the class.
Homework/Assignment
As an assignment, the students will create a digital story introducing themselves, expressing their
likes and dislikes, and making simple inquiries about their school or hobbies. The teacher explains
how to use digital story creation tools to create a simple digital story using text, images, and icons.
The focus should be on using the target phrases from the lesson (e.g. “My name is...”, “I like...” or “I study
at...”). The teacher will assess the digital stories based on the following criteria: the effectiveness of the
target phrases used, creativity, language accuracy, and overall presentation quality. The guideline
of the homework is shared with the students (Annex-8).
