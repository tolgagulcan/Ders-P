Activities
Before the Lesson
In this lesson, the students are expected to review the content at home before coming to class. It is
aimed to enhance the students’ active participation and deeper learning by using the flipped learning
approach. The teacher prepares a variety of digital resources to increase student motivation and
foster active participation. First, the teacher shares the Chores video link from EBA with the students for
pre-class study. To support their preparation, the teacher creates an activity titled Chores Worksheet
using one of visual creation and editing tools on key vocabulary items from the video. The students
complete this worksheet at home to familiarise themselves with the topic and key terms, enabling
them to actively participate in classroom activities. The teacher prepares an activity titled Chores
Match-Up using a web tool for matching activities to reinforce key vocabulary, increase student
motivation, and provide an interactive, gamified practice during the lesson (Annex-1).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
To activate the prior knowledge, review key vocabulary, and prepare the students for the main lesson
on Chores, the teacher asks a few quick, general questions to get the students thinking about the
topic, such as: “Which chores do you do at home? Who usually does the laundry in your family? Some
people think children should not do chores. Do you agree or disagree? Why? Do chores help children
become more responsible? Why, or why not?”
While Activity
The students bring the Chores Worksheet they completed at home, focusing on key vocabulary items
(e.g. wash dishes, take out the trash, make the bed, vacuum, do the laundry, tidy up). The teacher
goes through the worksheet with the class, discussing each word, checking sentences, and clarifying
any misunderstandings. The students are encouraged to actively participate and compare their
sentences with classmates and receive support and feedback to reinforce vocabulary and correct
usage. After the worksheet activity, the teacher initiates the Chores Match-Up activity in which the
students match the words with their correct meanings (Annex-1). Gamification is used to increase
the students’ motivation and reinforce key vocabulary, encourage active participation, and provide
immediate feedback.

Post-Activity
Following the activity, the students work in pairs to discuss a specific topic about their daily chores
using 3–4 vocabulary words from the lesson. During the discussion, the students ask and answer
questions such as: “Can you describe a chore you find easy and one you find difficult?”, “Even if you
don’t like a chore, which one do you still have to do?”, “How often do you do this chore?”, and “Which
chore do you dislike the most? How do you feel when you do it?” The teacher guides the conversation,
monitors speaking, and provides immediate feedback. These questions help the students reach the
objective by practising how to express and understand obligations, as well as likes and dislikes. The
objective is to strengthen speaking skills, encourage active use of vocabulary, and build confidence in
communicating about everyday routines.
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares a digital story titled Sharing Chores at Home using an interactive presentation
tool on family chores, along with group discussion materials and visual aids (Annex-2). The objective is
to engage the students, provide a clear context for the topic, and facilitate collaborative learning.
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
To activate prior knowledge, review key vocabulary, and prepare the students for the main lesson on
chores, the teacher shows the first scene of the digital story Sharing Chores at Home and asks a few
quick, critical thinking questions to get the students thinking about the topic such as: “What do you
notice first about this kitchen? Does this kitchen look like your kitchen at home? If you were Tom, how
would you respond to your mother’s request? Would it be different from the story?”
While Activity
Subsequent to the activity, the teacher displays the digital story Sharing Chores at Home to the class
(Annex-2). The students are required to watch the story carefully, paying attention to the family’s
interactions. After watching, the teacher asks the students to work in small groups to discuss how the
family could share the chores and decide which chore each family member should do. The teacher’s
objective is to engage the students in collaborative discussion, encourage critical thinking about the
story, and guide the group work. Following the story viewing and group discussion, the teacher initiates
a group presentation activity, in which the students are required to decide how the family members
could share the chores fairly. Each group prepares a short presentation or chart showing their
suggested distribution of chores, by using an interactive presentation tool. The objective of this activity
is to promote collaborative problem-solving, critical thinking, and speaking skills, while connecting the
story to the students’ own experiences and ensuring active participation in a meaningful, interactive
learning environment.

Problem solving
Before the Lesson
The teacher designs various visuals titled A House in Chaos using an interactive presentation tool to
grab the students’ attention and stimulate discussion about household responsibilities. The teacher
sets up a Chores Wheel game using one of the gamified measurement and evaluation web tools
to review common chores (Annex-3). The teacher also prepares a grouping activity titled Home
Responsibilities vs. School Responsibilities using one of the web tools for matching activities (Annex-4).
3. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher displays the visuals titled A House in Chaos and asks attention-grabbing questions such
as: “What can you see in the picture? Do you think this house looks organized or messy? Why? What
would you do if this were your house?”
While Activity
The teacher organises the students into small groups and explains that they will play a game to
review common household chores. The teacher initiates the Chores Wheel on the interactive board
and provides clear instructions, telling the students that when the wheel spins, a definition of a chore
will appear, and they must guess the correct term (Annex-3).The teacher manages the game by
spinning the wheel or allowing each group to take turns. The students discuss the definition within
their group and agree on the answer. Each correct answer earns the group a point, creating a friendly
competition among groups. This activity continues until all definitions on the wheel have been used,
or until a set time is reached. Through this competitive and interactive format, the students practise
listening comprehension, speaking, and collaborative skills while learning about daily household
responsibilities.

Post-Activity
Following the activity, the teacher displays a list of responsibilities on the interactive board,Home
Responsibilities vs. School Responsibilities, to help the students distinguish between home and school
responsibilities, while practising critical thinking and active participation (Annex-4). The students take
turns to come to the board and classify each task as either ‘Home’ or ‘School’. Alternatively, they can
call out their answers while the teacher moves the items on the board.
Before the Lesson
The teacher creates a poster titled Robotic Chores in the Future using a visual creation and editing tool
to grab the students’ attention and stimulate discussion.
4. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the lesson by presenting the poster titled Robotic Chores in the Future, which
features smart home devices and robots. The students are asked guiding questions such as: “How do
these robots change the way people manage household tasks, and what are the potential advantages
and disadvantages? If you could design a robot to do any chore at home, what would it be, and how
would it improve your daily life?” This activity encourages the students to analyse current technologies,
evaluate their benefits and drawbacks, and creatively imagine future applications, thereby fostering
critical thinking and problem-solving skills.
While Activity
The teacher divides the class into small groups. Each group works together to create a role-play script
about a ‘Future Smart Home’. The students imagine a family scene where robots or smart devices help
with chores (e.g. a robot cooking dinner, a smart fridge ordering groceries, or a robot ironing clothes).
By using a visual creation and editing tool, the students prepare virtual backgrounds to make their
role-play more realistic. They write dialogues where characters interact with robots, negotiate tasks,
and discuss the advantages or funny problems of having robots at home. The teacher explains how
the students will use digital tools to create virtual backgrounds and role-play scripts about a ‘Future
Smart Home’, giving examples and tips to help them get started. This helps the students focus and
understand household chores in a new way, use the right vocabulary, and be ready for creative group
activities.
Post-Activity
Each group presents their role-play performance to the class, using their prepared digital backgrounds.
After each presentation, classmates provide short peer feedback. The teacher summarises the
students’ contributions and provides short feedback from real-life technology trends.

Homework/Assignment
The students are asked to create a short video at home, showing how chores are shared amongst
family members. They can film themselves, their sisters/brothers, or parents performing daily tasks
such as making the bed, washing dishes, tidying up, or taking out the trash. The video should be
2–3 minutes long and include at least 4–5 vocabulary words learnt in the class. The students are
encouraged to be creative by adding narration, captions, or background music to explain the chore
distribution. This activity allows the students to apply classroom learning in real-life contexts, practise
English in authentic situations, and develop their digital storytelling skills.
