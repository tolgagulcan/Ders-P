Activities
Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares a Saving Our Planet presentation using one of the interactive presentation
tools (Annex-1).
1. Ders (Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher initiates the lesson by writing “Saving the Planet” on the board and inviting the students
to contribute their ideas on the subject. As the students offer suggestions such as recycling,
planting trees, using less water, and renewable energy, the teacher emphasises the significance of
environmental issues and the necessity of collaborative solutions.
While Activity
The teacher shares the key vocabulary and terms related to environmental protection on the board.
Then the students are asked to read these words aloud in English so that the teacher can assess their
pronunciation and familiarity with the vocabulary. The teacher then gives a detailed explanation
of the meaning and use of each term in the context of environmental protection, using English to
reinforce language acquisition. At this stage, the teacher initiates the Saving Our Planet presentation
on the interactive whiteboard and explains the importance of water conservation, environmental
protection, and wildlife preservation (Annex-1). Then the teacher initiates Vocabulary Exercise 1, 2, 3:
Saving the Planet on EBA and asks the students to complete them together.
Post-Activity
The teacher asks the students to complete the Saving the Planet test on EBA. At the end of the lesson,
the teacher asks the students to bring a stick for the next class and explains that they will use it in an
activity.

Before the Lesson
The teacher prepares the four colour-coded bins, labels them appropriately. Additionally, the teacher
creates a self-assessment form to be used at the end of the lesson (Annex-2).
2. Ders (IT Class)
Pre-Activity
The teacher engages the students in a brainstorming session on the topic of “Saving Our Planet”.
The teacher asks the students to think critically about the importance of environmental protection
and sustainability. Following this discussion, the teacher guides the students in writing effective
slogans that reflect their thoughts on these issues. The teacher encourages the students to think
creatively and emphasises the need for their slogans to be both memorable and motivational. Once
the students have developed their slogans, they share them with the class and receive feedback
from their peers and the teacher.
While Activity
The teacher informs the students that they will create a poster based on the slogans they developed
during the warm-up activity. The posters should raise awareness about environmental protection,
focusing on the specific issues they have chosen. The teacher explains that the students will use
one of the poster and flyer creation tools to design their posters. The students are encouraged to
choose images that complement their slogans, ensuring that the overall design of the poster is in
line with the environmental theme. When the posters are finished, the teacher helps the students to
print them. The students then attach their posters to sticks to form banners. The teacher reminds
the students that these banners will be used in the next lesson, possibly for a school event, thus
reinforcing the importance of their message.
Post-Activity
The teacher introduces four colour-coded bins, each for a specific type of recyclable material:
blue for paper, yellow for plastic, green for glass and grey for metal waste. The teacher labels each
bin with the corresponding type of material by writing the words paper, plastic, glass and metal
on the computer, then prints out the labels and sticks them on the bins. In the following lesson, the
teacher informs the students that these bins will be placed in the school garden. The students are
instructed to collect rubbish from the school garden, systematically sort the rubbish according to
its material type and then dispose of it in the appropriately labelled bins. This activity reinforces the
importance of proper waste management and recycling and promotes environmental awareness
and responsibility among the students. At the conclusion of the lesson, the teacher distributes
self-assessment forms to the students. They thoughtfully complete these forms, reflecting on their
learning and participation, and then return them to the teacher for review (Annex-2).

Before the Lesson
As the students will perform in the school garden with banners about saving the planet, the teacher
informs the students in advance. So, the teacher should make sure the posters are ready and there
are four colour-coded bins (blue for paper, yellow for plastic, green for glass, grey for metal). Before
the lesson, the teacher hides key vocabulary around the garden.
3. Ders (School Garden)
Pre-Activity
The students take part in a scavenger hunt in the school garden, where the teacher hides words
related to sustainability, recycling, and nature. Working in teams, the students race against the clock
to find as many words as possible. The team that finds the most words wins. This activity promotes
engagement with environmental issues and encourages teamwork and rapid decision-making.
While Activity
The students place their banners in the school garden as guided by the teacher and put on gloves
in accordance with the safety protocol for the activity. The teacher places the rubbish bins, labelled
for paper, plastic, glass and metal, in the garden. The teacher and the students then work together to
collect the rubbish from the garden. The teacher divides the class into four groups, each representing
a specific colour: blue for paper, yellow for plastic, green for glass and grey for metal. Each group is
responsible for sorting and collecting the rubbish in the appropriate bins. The teacher monitors the
time and the group that has correctly sorted and collected the most rubbish in the allotted time
is announced the winner. Once the horticultural maintenance was done, the rest of the students
and teaching staff gathered in the garden. The students present banners and slogans about
environmental protection. They also explain how to use the colour-coded bins correctly.
Post-Activity
The teacher records the students’ poster-making process and the garbage collection competition,
then uploads the videos to the school website with the slogan, “We are the planet protectors!”
Homework/Assignment
The students must create a fact sheet about climate change. It should cover causes, effects and
solutions. The students can present their fact sheet as a booklet, poster, or digital presentation. It
should have a clear title, well-organised sections, and relevant visuals. The completed fact sheet is
due by the next lesson. The students must post their assignments on EBA. The teacher will assess the
homework on EBA for accuracy, clarity, and creativity. The students can ask questions on EBA.
